# Will the plan window last? Two forecasts tried, one kept (2026-10-24)

`desk runway` answers one question from a tool's own readings: the percent of the plan window used, when it resets, and whether it will run out before then. The data is this machine's Codex history: 110 completed 5-hour windows on the plus plan (1,565 readings), between April and June 2026. Every number below was produced by a script in `scripts/` that anyone with the same ledger can rerun.

## The forecast that failed

The first version assumed the last hour's pace would continue to the reset, turned tokens into percent with the fitted rate, and gave the range between the slowest and fastest past windows' rates. `scripts/backtest-pace-projection.ts` stands inside each past window, uses only what was known then and compares with how the window ended: **the final percent fell inside the range in 2 of 26 cases (8%), where the range was meant to hold the middle 80%.** The middle projection was 31 points away on average. Use comes in bursts, so a pace does not persist for hours, and the interval around the rate could not carry that. It was removed from the product.

## The forecast that replaced it

Look at the past windows of the same plan and length that stood at the same stage (share of the window elapsed) with a similar percent, one reading per window, take the 10 closest, and report how many of them ended at 95% or more and where they ended (`src/desk/limits/analog.ts`). The window now running and anything after the moment asked about are never part of the past. With fewer than 30 past windows it forecasts nothing and says so.

`scripts/backtest-analog.ts` checks it three ways. Brier score is the mean squared gap between the forecast chance of ending at 95% or more and what happened; lower is better, and the base rate alone is the score to beat.

| Check | Readings asked | Brier (forecast / base rate) | Final percent inside the forecast's 10th to 90th percentile (aimed at 80%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Each window left out in turn, 10 neighbours | 304 | 0.169 / 0.289 | 81% |
| **Each window in time order, forecast only from the windows before it, 10 neighbours (how the product is used)** | 237 | **0.186 / 0.327** | **81%** |
| Same, 5 neighbours | 237 | 0.183 / 0.327 | 67% |
| Same, 20 neighbours | 237 | 0.218 / 0.327 | 86% |
| Same, only the 40 most recent windows | 237 | 0.210 / 0.307 | 86% |
| Same, adding the last hour's use to what "looks like this one" means | 237 | 0.191 / 0.327 | 83% |

Reading it, rolling forward in time with 10 neighbours: when the forecast said the chance was under 20%, 8 of 77 readings ended at 95% or more (10%); at 20% to 50%, 46 of 107 (43%); at 50% to 80%, 41 of 53 (77%). The chances track what happened. The last hour's use adds nothing (0.191 against 0.186), so it is not used. Ten neighbours was chosen because it kept the interval at its aim of 80%; five neighbours scored slightly better and held 67%.

## What did not hold

A forecaster that **stops learning** fails. When the earlier 55 windows were the whole past and the later 55 were forecast from them without updating, the Brier score was 0.414 against 0.433 for the base rate, every forecast chance was under 20% while 47% of those readings ended at 95% or more, and the interval held 44%. Usage in the later windows was heavier than in the earlier ones, and nothing in the earlier windows resembled it. The product keeps adding each finished window to its past, which is the rolling check above, but a change in how someone uses the plan will be under-forecast until the new windows have accumulated. The check that leaves windows out at random is the most flattering of the three, because a window is forecast from windows of its own era; the time-ordered check is the one to quote.

## Not covered

One person, one tool (Codex), one plan with enough windows (plus, 5-hour). The weekly window has 15 windows and cannot meet the 30-window floor; the free, team and unknown-plan windows have fewer still. Claude Code has no readings yet. The setting of 10 neighbours and the 20-minute freshness rule were tried on this same data, so the table is not a held-out test of those choices. The percent is in whole points and other devices on the same plan move it without a trace in these logs. A window's "final" percent is its last reading, which can come before the reset.
