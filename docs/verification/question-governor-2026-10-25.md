# The value-of-information question governor (2026-10-25)

Feature DF-107. An owner who is interrupted for a question that cannot change anything learns to answer without reading. So before the Desk asks, it works out what the answer is worth, and interrupts only if that is more than the interruption costs.

## What it does

- **What an answer is worth.** The question is "is this commit from this session?" with the Desk unsure. A yes makes the link explicit and the session takes the commit's state, worked out by the same code that computes landing (`outcomeOf`), with the session's other links in place. A headline figure moves only when that state is landed (merged or kept) or reverted; for a commit that is only committed, nothing moves and the worth is zero. The worth is the chance of a yes times the session's estimated spend. The chance is the share of the owner's own earlier answers once there are ten, by Laplace's rule so that ten yeses in ten is not certainty; one half before that, which the question says it is.
- **What an interruption costs.** 20 seconds to read and 180 to get back to work, at the owner's rate (33,333 micro-dollars a second, $120 an hour), times one plus the attention load of the gate (DF-088): the larger of agents working and questions asked against their limits, over everyone the gate has seen. The same question is worth less interrupting for when four agents are running.
- **The rule.** Ask now only if the worth is more than the cost; a tie waits. `desk questions ask` goes through only those; the rest wait for the weekly review, unlinked, which is counted as neither landed nor waste. `--all` asks every one, as before.
- **Fails safe.** If the gate's record exists and cannot be read, the load is unknown and every question waits, rather than being asked unpriced. If there is no gate on the machine, nothing is being watched and the load is zero, and the output says so.
- **On the record.** Each decision is a row in `question_decision` (schema 23): the question, when, ask now or queue, the worth, the cost, the load, the chance of yes, and the reason in words. The weekly review reports how many open questions would move a headline figure, the most any one is worth, and how many were held back from interrupting the owner that week and are still open.

## What ran

- `tests/desk/questions-governor.test.ts` (21): the cost at zero, half and full load and a negative load not being a discount; a tie queuing; the same answer asked about at zero load and not at full; an unreadable load and an unknown worth each queuing with the reason; the reason text; the chance of yes at nine answers (a half) and at ten and thirty (Laplace); the policy wait cost; against a real git repository, a session whose answer moves real money asked about and one that moves cents held back at the same load, everything held back with no load reading, the big session dropping out once the owner is loaded past its worth, a commit that is only committed never asked about, the terminal loop asking one and saying what waits and why, a loop with nothing worth asking saying so and not waiting for input, an answer still only linking and git still saying what the commit became, the decision log with its figures, the held-back count ending when a question is answered, writes that roll back if one fails, and the load reader on no gate, a gate file that cannot be read, and a real gate with three sessions for two people (3 of 4).
- `tests/desk/ui/week-questions-ui.test.ts` (1) through the real server and client, and the existing question, review, figure-class, help and migration tests updated for the new fields and schema 23.
- **On a copy of the sample ledger, through the command:** 35 open questions; `desk questions ask` offered 9 and held back 26, saying `26 wait for the weekly review, the most valuable worth $6.28 against $6.67 to interrupt you (no gate is running here, so no agent is being watched and the load is taken as zero)`. The first question offered was a $50.63 session.

## Not covered

- Only the attribution question is governed. The specification also lists policy approvals and canary overrides as question types; the gate's approval requests block an agent and are always asked, and a proposal already waits for the weekly review, so there was nothing to hold back. `policyWaitCost` (the saving a day of waiting forgoes) is built and tested and is not yet on a screen.
- The worth is the session's spend times the chance, not the full counterfactual of replaying every policy under yes and under no; the specification's version adds the landed work a policy would block, which only matters once a question changes a policy's verdict. No question does that yet.
- The chance of yes is the owner's overall rate on questions of this kind, not a per-method rate; with under ten answers it is one half. It was not checked against how often the owner's yes actually follows a given method.
- The cost figures (20 s to read, 180 s to recover, $120 an hour) are the specification's. How long a person takes to recover from an interruption varies a great deal between people and was not measured here.
- Held-back counts need the loop to have run: a question the owner never saw through `desk questions ask` has no decision row, though it is still listed as open.
- The real ledger on this machine was not run through `desk questions ask`: it has 3 repositories scanned and 3 of 428 sessions with a known landing, so it has few questions. The run above is a copy of the sample ledger.
