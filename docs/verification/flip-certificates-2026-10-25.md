# The one fact that would flip a verdict (2026-10-25)

Feature DF-068 built. A verdict is a call made from data, and how firm it is shows in what it would take to change it. A flip certificate is the smallest single change found that turns a verdict into another, or a plain statement that none was found among the changes tried. It is a search over a fixed list of candidates, each a change to one fact, and it says how many candidates it tried.

## What it covers

- **The canary's verdict** (card on the Brief, the Canary screen, `desk canary`): each task left out in turn, the control arm left out. The others keep their places, so the held-out half does not shift. The task that moved most is tried first, so a search cut short has looked where a flip is likeliest. The constructed scenario's regression rests on its control arm and on no task: `Leave the control arm out, and the verdict goes from regressed to inconclusive (no control arm ran, so a platform cause cannot be excluded). All 53 candidate changes were tried.`
- **A month's reconciliation against an invoice** (the Bill screen, `desk bill`): each model priced as a second source prices it, one at a time and all at once. The status is now a pure function of the invoice, the estimate, the tolerance and the coverage (`decideStatus`), so each candidate is that function asked again with the estimate moved by what the other price is worth in that month's tokens. The column appears only when a second price source was read.
- **A policy proposal's recommendation** (`desk propose` report, the decision record's limits): each paused held-out session twice, its landing state moved one step and its pause taken away. The rule is `adoptionFailures`, now one function that the decision record and the flip check both use, so they cannot disagree. On the sample ledger: `No single change flips this recommendation (keep the policy in force). All 20 candidate changes were tried.` (5 of 333 landed sessions blocked; moving one landing leaves 4).
- A regression that moves on one task was already withheld (`rests_on_one_task`); the certificate now names that task and what the verdict becomes without it.

## Defects found on the way

- The Bill screen said `billed: none. No invoice is imported` directly above a table showing a billed figure, when an invoice was imported but had no line dated inside the period. It now says how many invoices are imported and that none has a line inside the period.
- My first test of the one-task case assumed a baseline of 0.9 would leave the other tasks quiet; at that rate several tasks move 30 points in a 7-day window by chance, so the one-task rule never applied. A baseline of 0.97 makes the premise true.

- **A canary that passes everything could never be called `steady`.** The prove check for the one-task case first failed because, with every other task passing every day, the observed spread of the tasks' changes is exactly zero, so the interval has no width and the detectable change is zero, and the verdict was `inconclusive: interval too wide` with an interval of width 0. A strong model on easy tasks would sit there for ever. The detectable change now has a floor, the noise a pass-or-fail score cannot be without (each task's baseline rate with one pass and one failure added); a window with no observed spread is `steady` with a detectable change of about 5 points, which the card states. New tests: a model that passes every task every day is `steady` with a detectable change between 2 and 10 points (it failed before the floor and passes with it), one that fails everything the same way is `steady`, a drop to 60% from a baseline of all passes is still called `regressed`.
- **What the floor cost, measured on the canary simulation (300 histories, seed 20261022, days 28 to 90):** the rows that decide a `regressed` call are identical to the digit (false alarms 2 of 300, called by day 28, 42 and 90, median days, platform downgrades). What moved is `steady`: on histories with no change the share of days saying `steady` rose from 36.2% to 42.7%, and the share saying `steady` while a real change exists rose from 11.9% to 14.6% at a 5-point drop on every task, 2.1% to 2.8% at 8 points, 0.1% to 0.2% at 11 points, and 10.5% to 12.0% for an 11-point drop on half the tasks. `steady` does not mean nothing changed; it means no change as large as the detectable change is compatible with the data, and the card states that change. The simulation note now has section 8 with these shares, computed by the script; the before figures come from the same script at commit 7f09668 (to reproduce them, replace `Math.max(est.se, Math.sqrt(floorSum) / d.length)` with `est.se` in `src/desk/canary/verdict.ts` and run the script again).

## Not covered

- **The candidates are a fixed list, not every possible change.** A verdict can flip on a change that is not on the list (two tasks at once, a different tolerance, a different window length). `No single change flips` means none of the listed ones did, and the sentence says how many were tried.
- **The reconciliation flip uses the one second price source** the Desk reads (Portkey's records); with none, there is no flip. A different disputed price that no source offers is not tried.
- **Landing state is one step of a two-state ladder** (landed or not), because the replay records a boolean. A finer ladder (pull request, merge, release) would need the replay to carry it.
- The proposal's flip covers the held-out period only; the in-sample period is shown beside it and is not tested.
- The canary search stops after 8 seconds and then says so. How long it takes on a canary of hundreds of tasks was not measured; the sample's 52 tasks finish inside a test run.
- The dossier (`canary_evidence`, `policy_change`) carries the verdict and not the flip sentence, because a dossier's claims are recomputed by the verifier and a flip claim has no recomputation yet.
