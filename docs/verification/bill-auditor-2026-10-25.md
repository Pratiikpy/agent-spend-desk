# The bill auditor and the dispute pack (2026-10-25)

Feature DF-062. A vendor that bills per outcome (a resolution, a completed task, a credit) decides for itself what a success is. `desk invoices audit` holds each billed line to the owner's own definition of one, and can write a signed pack to put to the vendor.

## What it does

- **The definition.** A JSON file the owner writes (`--definition`): the word for one outcome, which invoice lines are per-outcome lines (a case-insensitive expression), whether a line's quantity or the line itself is the number of outcomes, which landing states count as success (committed, merged or kept; a reverted session never counts), the fewest calls a session needs, and how many backed lines to draw for a hand check. Every field has a default, a field that is present and wrong is refused, and so is one that is not known. Without a file the default is used and the output says so.
- **The judgement.** For each UTC day, the sessions in the owner's logs that met the definition are given out one to a billed outcome, the session that ends soonest first (which maximises how many outcomes can be backed). A session spans its first call to its last, so one that runs past midnight can back an outcome on either day, but only one. Each line then says how many of its outcomes are backed and how many are not, and the share of its amount the unbacked ones make up. Every line is judged; a seeded draw of the backed ones is printed for a hand check.
- **What it will not judge, and says so.** A credit or refund, a quantity that is not a whole number of outcomes, a line with no quantity when quantities are what count, a line with no date; invoices on hold for a sum mismatch; lines dated outside the month. Each appears in the output with the lines and dollars it covers.
- **Duplicates.** The same day, description, quantity, model and amount appearing more than once across the vendor's invoices for the month, with the extra copies priced. Two real charges can look the same, so the output says to ask.
- **Loops and failures.** The Desk's two rules that need no judgement about the work (the same tool error repeated three times; output cut off and redone) are summed by day against that day's estimated model spend. The share of a day's charge that corresponds is itemised as an estimate that assumes the charge follows the model spend, and is left out of the disputed figure, since a vendor may bill a failed attempt by its own terms.
- **It cannot run** when no invoice covers the month, when every invoice is on hold, when the invoices mix currencies, when no line looks like a per-outcome line, or when no per-outcome line carries a whole-number quantity. Each says so, and names what would help.
- **The dispute pack** (`--pack DIR`): a dossier of kind `dispute_pack`, audience counterparty, redacted, signed with the owner's key and checked by the offline verifier. Its rows are the vendor's own lines with the verdicts, one row per day (sessions, qualifying sessions, outcomes charged and backed) and the duplicates. Every row is built from a short list of fields, and a row with any other field, text over 200 characters, a line break, a path or code is refused before anything is written. The sums over the rows are claims the verifier recomputes.

## What ran

- `tests/desk/billing-audit.test.ts` (22), against a real ledger of nine sessions on known days and a real imported CSV invoice, with every expectation worked out by hand first: the allocation day by day (two of four backed on one day, one of two on another because a session was too small, an overnight session backing the 14th and not the 15th, a day with no session at all), the totals (10 outcomes, 5 backed, $5.00 of $10.00 unbacked), what was not judged and why, the earliest-end rule against a short session and an overnight one, committed counting only if the definition says so, one outcome per line, the seeded draw, a duplicate priced, the loop share computed from that day's own spend, each way the audit cannot run, the text, the pack passing the verifier, the pack holding no session id, project path, prompt text or error text, the verifier refusing a changed row, the redaction rules one by one, and the command including a second pack refused in a folder that is not empty.
- Through the real command line (`desk invoices audit --pack`), then the stand-alone verifier on the written pack: `RESULT: PASS`.
- `desk prove`, check `bill-audit-matches-an-independent-matching-and-the-pack-verifies`: on the sample ledger, five days are charged two outcomes over what the qualifying sessions could back; the audit's backed count equals a maximum matching worked out by augmenting paths, a different method from the audit's; the pack passes the offline verifier, carries no session id or project path of the ledger, and is refused when a row is changed.

## Not covered

- The Desk sees the owner's side only: the agent tools whose logs it reads. An outcome the vendor produced where the Desk cannot see (a customer chat the owner's agent never touched) looks like one with nothing behind it. Every disputed figure is therefore a claim to put to the vendor, and the output and the pack say so.
- No real vendor's per-outcome export was available, so the importer was exercised on CSV invoices built to the generic column names the Desk reads, not on a named vendor's file. A vendor whose export has no per-line quantity or date needs `"unitsFrom": "line"` or cannot be audited.
- A line is matched to sessions by day, not by an identifier shared with the vendor; a vendor export that carries the owner's own session or conversation id could be matched exactly, and is not.
- The definition is about where the work went (committed, merged, kept). A success the owner defines another way (a customer reply, a ticket closed) is outside what the ledger knows.
- Days are UTC. A vendor that bills by another time zone will shift lines across midnight.
- The verifier recomputes the sums over the pack's rows. It does not recompute how outcomes were given out to lines; the day counts are in the pack so a person can redo that by hand.
- Nobody on the vendor's side has read a pack, so whether a counterparty finds it usable is not known.
