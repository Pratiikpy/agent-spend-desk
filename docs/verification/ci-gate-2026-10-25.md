# The CI gate: fail a change that worsens the canary suite or raises its cost, and never call what the statistic does not show (2026-10-25)

Feature DF-060 built. `desk ci --baseline FILE --candidate FILE` is the step a pipeline calls; `desk ci --print-workflow` prints a GitHub Actions job that uses it.

## The contract

Two files of canary runs: the **baseline** (recorded on the main branch, `data/desk/canary/runs.jsonl`) and the **candidate** (the same tasks run on the change: `desk canary run --runs candidate.jsonl`, with the change's model settings in the environment). The two are compared by paired per-task differences, with the canary's own interval: the task is the unit, the kinds of task are the clusters, 99% coverage.

Exit codes: **0 pass, 1 fail, 2 could not run, 3 inconclusive**. Inconclusive is never a pass. `--on-inconclusive fail` makes it 1, and `--on-inconclusive pass` makes it 0 with a line saying that is not a pass; a fail is never softened.

| Quality | When |
|---|---|
| fail | the interval for the change in pass rate lies wholly below zero, the change is at least the smallest the canary calls (3 points, `--min-delta`), and the held-out half of the tasks does not move the other way |
| pass | the interval's lower end is no lower than minus that smallest change: a drop that size is ruled out at 99% |
| inconclusive | anything else: fewer than 12 tasks scored on both sides, 5% or more of the candidate's attempts with no score, a harness that changed (the tasks or their checks differ), no baseline, or an interval that cannot rule out a drop and does not show one |

| Cost (only with `--max-cost-increase`) | When |
|---|---|
| fail | the interval for the relative change in cost per attempt lies wholly past the bound |
| pass | it lies wholly within the bound |
| inconclusive | it straddles the bound; not evaluated, with the reason, when a model has no price or a run has no token count (never guessed) |

`--evidence DIR` writes `ci-gate.json` (the verdict, the settings, the per-task table, and each input's path, row count and SHA-256) and `ci-gate.md` (the pull-request comment). `--format markdown` prints that comment, `--format json` the full result.

## What it does on the recorded canary

On the shipped 30-day scenario (a regression planted on day 15, `data/sample/canary/runs.jsonl`), against its first week:

- the pack run on day 15 **fails**: the pass rate fell 38.6 points (interval 53.2 to 24.0 points);
- the quiet second week is **inconclusive, not a pass**: the change is -1.1 points with an interval of -5.2 to +3.1, which does not rule out a 3-point drop. **One pack of the 52 tasks cannot certify a 3-point change at 99%; its interval is about 4 points either side.** With `--min-delta 0.06` the same week passes. The honest reading is that the gate certifies "no drop of at least X" only for an X the suite is large enough to show, and says which when it cannot;
- day 14, on which a tenth of the attempts produced no score, makes **no call** rather than reading a fall into the gaps.

## What it does when the truth is known

`src/desk/ci/simulate.ts` draws 400 pairs of packs (52 tasks, 5 repeats each) from task pass probabilities spread from 0.97 to 0.10, the candidate's lower by a stated drop on every task, and runs the gate:

| True fall | fail | pass | inconclusive |
|---|---|---|---|
| none | 1 (0.25%) | 11 | 388 |
| 3 points | 20 | 4 | 376 |
| 5 points | 44 | 2 | 354 |
| 10 points | 190 (47%) | 0 | 210 |
| 20 points | 383 (96%) | 0 | 17 |

With nothing changed it called a fall once in 400 (the interval's nominal rate is 1%). It almost never certifies "no drop" at this pack size (11 of 400; 55 of 400 at `--min-delta 0.06`; 29 of 400 with fifteen repeats instead of five), because a drop of 3 points is below what one pack can rule out. A 20-point fall is called 96% of the time, a 10-point fall about half the time, and a real fall is almost never passed. In short: it is safe against false alarms, and slow to certify. For a team that wants a gate that passes good changes, the lever is more repeats, a larger suite or a larger `--min-delta`, stated.

## What ran

`tests/desk/ci.test.ts` (28): a pass, a fail across the suite with the held-out half agreeing, a rise, a fall carried by one task of twenty (inconclusive: the interval cannot rule out no change), a fall the held-out half does not share, a wide interval, too few tasks, too many unscored attempts, a changed harness, nothing to compare, a change of served model noted, determinism; cost pass, fail, inconclusive, unpriced model, no bound, no price list, and quality and cost each able to fail alone; the command's exit codes, the exit-2 cases (missing and junk files, silly numbers, an unreadable price list), `--on-inconclusive`, the three output formats, the evidence pack with its input hashes, the cost gate through the price list, the workflow snippet; and the three recorded-canary cases above.

## Not covered

- **Not run in a real pipeline.** The workflow snippet is generated and its commands tested, but no GitHub Actions run was made; the candidate pack needs the pipeline's own model key and costs real tokens (about 260 attempts).
- The candidate pack needs `desk canary run --runs FILE` to be run on the change; `--runs` was added for this and is not exercised against a live model here.
- The spec's "cost per landed task": a canary task has no landing state, so cost is per scored attempt, stated as such.
- A change small enough to matter but smaller than the interval is always inconclusive on one pack. More repeats narrow it (the interval narrows with the square root of the repeats); a bigger suite narrows it further. Neither was measured.
- The study below is of packs drawn from stated pass probabilities, not of a real model; its spread of task difficulty (0.97 down to 0.10, most tasks in the middle) is more variable than the recorded canary's, so its intervals are wider than a real pack's and its power lower.
