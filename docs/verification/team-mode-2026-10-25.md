# Team mode (DF-059), 2026-10-25

Several people's spend, put together without any one of them handing over a log. Each person's own Desk writes a signed report of counts and dollars; one owner takes the reports in; each person sees the team at the level their role allows; every look is written down.

## What a report holds

`desk team export --person <id>` reads the ledger on the person's own machine and writes, for each day, the calls, tokens and list-price dollars by model, the calls and dollars by project label, and the dollars by outcome (merged, kept, committed, reverted, unlinked, none). It holds no prompt, no path, no session id, no commit and no branch. A project label is the last folder name of the project; `--anonymise` replaces each with a code. The report is signed with the person's Ed25519 key (a DSSE envelope, `application/vnd.desk.team-report+json`); the key is the one `desk team key` prints and is held outside the repository. `shapeProblem` refuses, on export and again on merge, any field that is not in the shape, so a report that carried text would not be written and one that arrived carrying text is not taken in.

## Who may see what

There are no passwords. A person is the holder of a key the owner has added (`desk team add-member <id> --key <file> --role <role>`), and the owner is whoever made the team. Roles are viewer, member, lead and owner, and a withdrawn member holds none.

The views are spend, models, projects, landing (viewer and above), me (member and above), people and roles (lead and above; people also needs the owner to switch per-person views on, which is off when a team is made), and access-log and settings (owner only). Only an owner can add a person, change a role, change a setting or take in reports; the last owner cannot be demoted or withdrawn. A role change is an event and none is ever edited (`team_roles` rejects `UPDATE` and `DELETE`). A refusal says which role was needed and which the person holds.

## What it will not do

- **Rank people.** The people view lists by name, whatever each spent; a request to sort it by spend is refused and logged; nothing in the code orders by a person's spend (a mutation that did so failed two tests).
- **Show a line one or two people stand behind.** A model or project used by fewer people than the minimum group (3 unless the owner changes it) is folded into one "other" line, and a day with fewer people than that has its figures held back and is in the total only. A team smaller than the minimum shows its total and nothing else. The lines are in alphabetical order, not in order of cost.
- **Count a day twice.** When a person sends a later report for a day, it replaces their earlier one for that day.
- **Take in a report it cannot trust.** Refused: an altered payload, a report signed by a key outside the team or by a withdrawn member, a report that names someone other than its signer, an envelope that is not an envelope, and any with a field outside the shape. Each refusal is named in the merge output.

## The access log

Every request, allowed or refused, and every change an owner makes, is a line in the team database: when, who (by key, and by name when the key is a member's), which view, whether it was allowed and why. Each line carries the hash of the one before it, and `desk team verify-log` walks the chain and names the first line that does not follow. `UPDATE` and `DELETE` are rejected by triggers; a line changed behind the Desk's back by dropping the trigger is found by the chain.

## What was run

`tests/desk/team.test.ts` (34 tests) and `tests/desk/ui/team-ui.test.ts` (7) against a sample team of six whose answers are planted (`src/desk/sample/team.ts`: one person spends several times more than any other, one model and three projects are used by fewer than three people, one day has one worker): every role against every view; denial of a stranger's key, a withdrawn member, an unknown view; totals equal to the planted ones; folding and hiding; the people view in name order and unchanged when the top spender's report is removed; each of the refused reports above; the last-owner rule; the append-only triggers; the chain broken and found at the right line; a report made from a real ingested ledger with no path or session id in it; and the command line run as two people with two key files against one team file. `desk prove` runs the same as `team-reports-add-up-and-roles-refuse-what-they-do-not-hold`. Mutation checks: turning folding off failed 4 tests, giving viewers the access log failed 2, hiding days off failed 2, ranking people by spend failed 2.

The Team screen (`desk ui`, fourteenth) shows a person the views their role holds and lists the ones it closes with the role they need. Looked at in a browser on a desktop in light and on a phone in dark, as a lead and as an owner. In `--sample` the screen can be looked at as each of the six; live it is the machine's own key and nothing else, and a key that is not in the team sees nothing and the attempt is logged.

## Not covered

- More than one machine in real use. Everything above ran on one machine, with reports passed as files; no team has used it.
- A report's honesty. A report is signed by its sender, so it cannot be altered or sent in someone else's name; it can still be wrong, or made from a ledger the sender edited. The Desk does not audit a person's own ledger.
- Revoking a key. A withdrawn member's later reports are refused, and their earlier reports stay in the totals; removing them would need the owner to delete rows, which the Desk offers no command for.
- A hosted service, single sign-on, or a person who has lost their key. There is no server, and a lost key means the owner adds a new one under a new id.
- Small groups. The minimum group hides a figure that one or two people stand behind, but a person can still be inferred from a total with and without them over time, and the Desk does not defend against that.
- Currency and invoices. Figures are the list-price estimates of each person's own price list at the time; the team does not reconcile them with a bill.
- Time zones. A day is the day in the sender's ledger, so a team across time zones has days that do not line up exactly.
