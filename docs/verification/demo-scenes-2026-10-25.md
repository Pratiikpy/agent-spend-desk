# The demo as scenes, and every number moving (DF-014 to DF-019, DF-114)

`desk demo` runs offline in the order of the video: the opening ("Where did my limit go?", the limit autopsy), scene 0 (the month's headline, each dollar labelled as an estimate), scene 1 (the waste rules and the biggest block), scene 1b (the bill through Zetaris), scene 2 (the canary and its call), scene 3 (a cheaper route on the same task pack), scene 4 (propose, check, attack and replay on a month the draft never saw; the question asked with the engines on and off; approval by echoing the digest and the offline verifier, including a copy with one byte changed; the gate blocking a call) and the closing (run `desk` on your own logs; `desk demo --compare`). It finishes in about 35 seconds on this machine; `tests/desk/demo.test.ts` requires at most 230 seconds, the scene headings in order, the opening and closing text, the canary's call, and no missing recording.

`desk demo --compare` (and `--mutate SEED`) computes the figures the scenes stand on twice: on the recorded sample, and on a changed one (another seed with no tool or model change planted, and a canary file in which the served model never changed, made from the recorded one without altering it). On the recorded run all six figures moved: the estimated spend ($6,196.64 over 66,654 calls against $6,810.54 over 64,503), the waste found, the biggest block (C-REVERT $22.05 in one session against $68.81 in another), the canary (regressed, -40.0 points, against steady, +0.0), and the route comparison, which has no second model to compare in the changed file and says so.

Found while writing the test: the Ask scene printed `E_CASSETTE_MISS` twice, because the recordings of its two model replies no longer matched the request after the Ask router changed. They were re-recorded against the live model (Nemotron through NVIDIA's API); the replies in the demo are now the recorded ones again.

Not covered: the video itself is not recorded here; the scene order and captions are what the script follows. The timing budget is measured on one machine.
