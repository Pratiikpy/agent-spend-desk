# Precedent memory, and the Desk's use of the memory engine (2026-10-25)

Features DF-105 and DF-116. `desk precedents list | sync | track | show | cite`, `desk memory list | show | feedback | mine | sleep | restore`, `desk remember ... --replaces ID`, a section in `desk propose`, and an override in `desk landing`.

## What it does

- **Precedents.** Each policy change the owner approved becomes a precedent: the rule-by-rule diff in words (`day-cap: day cap $10.00 to $15.00`), what the wind tunnel expected of it (a range and the sessions it rested on, and whether history may not predict), the policy as adopted, and a memory in the engine. Schema 26. A later change to a rule an earlier precedent also changed replaces that precedent: the older memory is marked superseded and kept, and still ranks, penalised, under the engine's formula. It is made when `desk approve` succeeds, or by `desk precedents sync`.
- **Outcomes, at 7, 14 and 30 days.** For each horizon that has fully passed in the ledger, the adopted policy is replayed on the sessions that followed and the result is stored once: sessions, sessions paused, calls asked and denied, dollars not spent, landed runs and how many it would have blocked. These are replays, not the gate's record of what it stopped, and the sentence says "replayed". The ledger's own last day is the present when it is later than the clock.
- **Shown to a proposal.** `desk propose` puts the words of its change to the memory and prints the closest precedents (at most three, above the engine's 0.35 on the final score) with what each did, in this form (the figures are an example): "On 2026-09-20 you approved: day-cap: day cap $100.00 to $5.00. Since then (30 days, replayed on the 74 sessions that followed) it paused 9 sessions, would have saved $41.00 and blocked none of the 90 landed runs." With nothing close it says "No historical precedent found." With `--ablate hmem` it says no memory was available; the citation is the difference.
- **The engine's own care of what it holds.** `feedback` moves a memory's confidence by the engine's fixed steps (+0.05, -0.03, -0.2) and the trust ledger records who; a memory called wrong falls out of a citation. `mine` takes durable statements out of text, by the engine's model-free rule or, with `--model`, through the same model route Ask uses and the engine's strict reader of the reply. `sleep` previews and runs the engine's consolidation.
- **Sleep cannot delete what is the owner's.** The engine's sleep archives any memory that is 30 days old, rarely recalled and unreferenced, and folds near-duplicates into one. That includes the owner's notes, a definition of what landed, and a precedent. The Desk holds the plan to what the engine mined itself; everything short-term may still be consolidated. The engine's own backup lives only in the process that made it, so the Desk writes the whole memory to `backups/sleep-<time>.json` first and `restore` works from another run.
- **Statements about authors change attribution.** "Commits by release-bot@acme.test are human" (or "agent work") outranks a Co-Authored-By trailer for that author when commits are linked to sessions: `desk landing` reads the live notes, applies them by the author's pseudonym (the ledger never holds the address), counts the commits it read differently, and lists any author two live notes disagree about without applying either. `--ablate hmem` leaves the memory out.

- `tests/desk/precedent.test.ts` (17): the diff in words for a cap, a threshold, an added and a removed rule; an approved decision becoming a precedent while proposed, denied and incomparable ones do not (with the reason); idempotent sync; a later change to the same rule replacing the earlier one in the ledger and the memory without deleting either, and a change to another rule not; waiting for a horizon (5 days left); the three horizons measured once and equal to a replay of the adopted policy on the window; the sentence; a citation for a proposal that touches the same rule and none for one that does not; a missing memory and a change that touches no rule; a precedent falling out of the answer after the owner calls it wrong; the commands; mining statements (with an injected generator) and nothing from small talk; confidence steps and the trust ledger; sleep never touching a note, a definition or a precedent that is old and unused, archiving only mined statements after a backup that a new run can restore, refusing a path-like backup id and a memory not on disk; the real command line.

- `tests/desk/author-rules.test.ts` (7): the statement read in the ways an owner says it, disagreeing notes not applied, a replaced note no longer applying, a commit with an agent trailer by a bot linked `trailer_window`/high without a rule and not with "human", unchanged for another author or a rule that agrees, and `desk landing` with and without `--ablate hmem` through the real command line.

## Defects the work found

- The first precedent text carried the forecast and scored below the engine's threshold for the very change it described; the memory now holds the change in the words a proposal will use, and the forecast stays with the precedent row.
- The engine's sleep would have archived the owner's own definition of what landed, and a precedent, after 30 days of non-use. Guarded as above.
- The engine's backup is process-local, so "desk memory restore" would have failed on any later run. Replaced by a file backup.

## Not covered

- **The embedder is the engine's deterministic hash embedder**, and the ranking is its hybrid score. A real embedder would mean changing the vendored engine; it was not. Relevance is therefore word overlap more than meaning, which is why the memory text is kept to the change.
- Outcomes are replays of the adopted policy on what followed. A policy the owner then edited by hand, or asks the owner approved, is not reflected; the gate's own record of what it stopped is not read.
- Precedents are made from the regret ledger's recorded decisions, so a policy change adopted without `desk propose --dossier` and `desk approve` is not a precedent.
- An author statement needs an email address, because a name matches nothing the ledger holds, and the Desk's only other sign of agent work is the trailer.
- Mining with a model sends the text the owner gave it to the model route, masked for secrets as every model call is; it was tested with an injected generator, not against the live model.
- The sleep cycle's synthesis of mined statements was exercised only to the extent of the guard; the engine's folding behaviour itself is the engine's.
