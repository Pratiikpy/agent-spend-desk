## 1. What Plumbline is

A **decision-intelligence kernel** with **domain packs**. The kernel turns fragmented, disputed or incomplete evidence plus rules into a controlled, auditable decision: it handles source access and evidence capture, document and structured-data understanding, entity resolution and temporal facts, claim verification and explanation, policy and memory, approvals and execution control, audit and outcome learning. A domain pack supplies entity types, per-attribute authority tables, connectors, policy rules, a gold-set generator, the precedent schema and the interface vocabulary.

**Procurement counterparty release is the first pack (the first to be designed and built in full).** A procurement or vendor-risk owner submits a case ("release this payment to this vendor"). Plumbline reconciles what internal systems, public registries and documents say about the vendor, verifies every material claim against stored source text, decides under deterministic policy and precedent with an approval gate, and hands back a decision dossier whose record a stranger can re-verify offline against the stored text layer (section 9a states what that does and does not cover). Further packs (supplier onboarding, invoice exceptions, third-party risk, contract compliance, investigations) share the kernel and the common thread: decisions made from evidence that is fragmented, disputed, incomplete or subject to rules. Not every domain belongs. Generality is a claim we test, not assume: a second, small pack is built on the same kernel before the kernel boundary is called stable.

The modules below are organised by capability and user workflow. Hackathon tracks, sponsor requirements and funder criteria are *compliance profiles* mapped onto them in `docs/design/03-compliance-profiles.md`; they do not define the product. Prateek has stated Track 3 (Reasoning Architecture) as the primary track; the product covers all three.

| Module | Job | Layer |
|---|---|---|

| M2 Document Intelligence | PDFs and scans to verifiable, locator-addressed text | kernel |

Why this problem (a premise to test, **NOT VERIFIED** until practitioner interviews): releasing money to a wrong or misrepresented counterparty is a recurring failure, the inputs plausibly conflict, and the steps have checkable answers, which lets us measure the agents instead of describing them. The claim that no single step reconciles identity facts at release time is the same unvalidated premise. Defensible impact figures, each with its definition (15 §2): FBI IC3 2025 total business email compromise losses of $3.05 billion (self-reported complaints, all BEC, not split by vendor change); the AFP survey fielded January 2026 on 2025 data, in which 74% of respondents were affected by BEC (the survey fielded in 2025 on 2024 data reported 63%); the ACFE 2026 median billing-scheme loss of $90,000 (occupational fraud by insiders, not vendor impersonation); the Finansinspektionen 2024 test of 19 banks on 5,000 UN and EU list names, in which mean recall on altered names was 63.9% against 86.3% as spelled (medians 73.4% and 99.3%; per-bank altered-name recall ranged from 5.0% to 96.1%; no bank reached 100%). **There is no primary-source cost figure for releasing payment to the wrong counterparty, and none is published.** The "85 to 95 per cent false positives" figure has no primary source and is not used.

## 1-update. Product positioning after the deep-research report (D-019, 2026-10-15)

The product is positioned as the **release gate for the irreversible actions AI agents take**, with the vendor-payment and bank-detail-change release as the hero pack (the user who pays: accounts-payable and finance-control leads) and the coding and infrastructure release gate as the second pack through the plugin interface (the largest audience). The kernel is unchanged. Changes to emphasis:

- The primary claim is deterministic: quotes checked against stored text without a model call, exact minimal-evidence and minimal-flip explanations, a restrict-only gate, and an override-to-rule loop in which a proposed rule is replayed on stored cases **and** checked symbolically for whether it widens the permissive surface (replay on a small case set can pass trivially when no stored case lies in the newly exposed region). The symbolic check moves from a bake-off candidate to a required promotion step.
- The jury of judges is demoted to an experimental, ablation-gated feature: multi-agent debate studies report sycophancy and agreement collapse (arXiv 2509.23055, 2604.02668; existence of the papers checked 2026-10-15, results not re-derived).
- Not positioned as an engineering evaluation or observability tool (Braintrust and Langfuse already convert production traces into regression sets in CI); the product is the runtime business-transaction gate.
- Headless first: webhook and MCP interfaces so finance teams are not asked to adopt another console; the console is for the approval queue and the proof view.
- The owner-operator persona is deferred: buyers who pay are finance, compliance and security leads; solo operators contain agents with free containers (evidence is anecdotal, to be tested by interviews).

## 1a. The submission slice, and how we climb past Level 3

The kernel is deliberately large. What a judge runs, watches and reads is one **narrow slice** executed to the end. The organizers' own guidance is explicit that their Level 3 ("High-risk") is not "more modules": it is a complex domain handled as "a narrow prototype rather than a platform", with a small curated dataset, strong caveats, a working fallback mode and no heavy training or simulation at run time, and it warns that these builds are "the easiest to under-deliver". It also names, as things to avoid, a platform for every problem in an industry, a full production dashboard, large data pipelines where a sample would do, and memory of everything forever. Prateek's decision is to build everything and to own every component (D-013), so the slice defines how the product is **presented and scored**, not how much is built: the build covers all modules, the submission leads with the slice, and on the organizers' scale we climb by depth and proof.

**One-sentence statement (organizer format).** We are building an agent that helps a procurement or accounts-payable approver decide whether to release a payment to a vendor by reconciling conflicting internal records, public registries and documents, verifying each claim against the source text, and applying the company's policy and precedent, producing a decision dossier with cited evidence, reasons, gaps, the one fact that would flip the answer and an approval state, and by turning the approver's overrides into rules only after they pass a replay on past cases and a symbolic check.

## 2. Principles (each answers a defect found in the prior art)

1. A citation is checkable. The model points to a span id; code supplies the quote and its hash, and a mechanical check confirms the quote sits at the cited locator. A fabricated quote cannot be expressed (14 §A1); that the span supports the claim is a recorded judgement, not a proof.
2. Models propose; code disposes. Rules, limits, vetoes and approvals are code; a proposal becomes an action only through a typed gate.
3. Conflict is data. Disagreement is stored with both sides, the rule that chose, and an `unresolved` state when no rule can choose.
4. Absence is not falsity. Evidence silent, evidence contradicting, source unavailable, source not checked: four different facts.
5. Failure is visible. No empty success, no fabricated default, no silent degradation.
6. Everything replays. Every external effect goes through a recorded port, so recorded runs replay exactly and deterministic stages replay on any input (section 4).
7. Untrusted text is data. It never becomes an instruction, a rule, or a fact the rule engine uses without promotion. Untrusted input can add holds and checks, never permission (restrict-only, section 5; 10 §D.4 and §F1).
8. Declare, do not infer: source lineage, authority tiers and independence are declared in data, not guessed by a model (07 §2).
9. Trust never rises: combining inputs yields the least trusted of them (a lattice meet), so mixing trusted and untrusted data never launders trust (section 5, 11 §6).
10. Memory is append-only; consolidation only proposes, and a proposal carries a machine-checkable certificate (11 §10).
11. We claim only what a test shows, and we say which cases and surfaces were not covered.

## 4. M7 Orchestration runtime (our kernel contracts on a measured substrate; `prior-art/06` §9)

Two layers. **Kernel contracts (ours, framework-independent):** ports for model, tools, clock and ids, plus the set the blueprints need (`net`, `fs`, `sandbox`, `journal`, secret ids (generated, never recorded), signer client, `keys`, `authorize`, `store`, `ledger`, `scheduler`, `human`, `approvals` (the approval-service port, implemented by M13), and a kill-state interceptor; `rng` is a kernel service derived from the seed and not a recorded port; owners and signatures are in the M7 blueprint, register section 2.2 and pass 3, INT-130 and INT-149); effects with record and replay cassettes keyed by effect slot with request-hash verification, and strict miss behaviour; hard budgets reserved before each call (reasoning tokens capped explicitly in the reservation) and charged for retries; one JSONL trace schema, owned by M7, in which every decision carries `why` (with `why_kind`, `decider`, `phase`, `agent`, `verb`, `prevLineHash`, `budget` and `tenant` fields); error classes (transient, validation, budget, policy, fatal); `Proposal<T>` to `Approved<T>` gates; trust-level propagation; certificates. `tenant` is a required field of the step context, the effect slot, ledger leaves, store names and cassette headers from the first line of code (the judge path runs one tenant, `tn_default`), so tenancy is never retrofitted. **Execution substrate (chosen by the bake-off in section 19a):** typed state and commits (`commit` is a pure kernel function every substrate calls), graph scheduling with bounded cycles (the graph model is series-parallel with bounded loops; a pack needing more extends the compiler), checkpoint and resume, fan-out merged in branch order with no race joins, and in-run human steps (a source's `input_required`, for example) as step-boundary interrupts that fail closed. Approval waiting is not a runtime interrupt: it is a case state with its own clocks (section 14a), and a decision run ends at `decision.final`. The substrate is a thin runtime of ours, LangGraph.js or XState, and Temporal only if a judge can run it with one command; bake-off 1 pre-registers a judge-run screen that Temporal is expected to fail (its testing package downloads a server) and XState to pass, both NOT VERIFIED until run. The guarantees are split by owner. **Our effect layer owns:** effect keys and idempotency (a re-executed step must not repeat a paid or external effect), the write-ahead effect log, cassettes, budget reservation, trace and `why`, gates and trust propagation. **The substrate owns:** scheduling, checkpoint persistence, fan-out merge order and interrupt delivery. Crash safety, order independence and fail-closed interrupts are therefore substrate behaviours that the effect layer must be shown to protect; every candidate substrate must pass the eight acceptance tests with our effect layer on top before it is chosen (D-009 rejected LangGraph's resume semantics as a default for exactly this reason).

**Canonicalisation.** RFC 8785 JCS with a restricted Plumbline profile (no floating-point numbers in any hashed, signed, recorded or cassetted document: floats exist only in memory, for scoring, ranking and estimation, and integer scales are named types, `Ppm`, `Milli`, `Micros`, `Cb`, with money as `MinorUnits`; four self-describing digest forms, `RawHash`, `DocHash`, `Commitment` and `TreeHash`; a `Commitment` has one fixed preimage layout (tag, length-prefixed context, 32-byte salt, value) with its salt scope declared per tag in a tag registry; file names use `pathOf`, never a colon), implemented and tested against published vectors and a second implementation (13 §4.5). **Contracts and validation.** Schemas are JSON Schema documents (a standard, exportable as MCP `outputSchema`, checkable from any language), typed in TypeScript through a schema-first library and validated by a pinned strict validator; the schema documents are hashed under JCS and golden-hash tested, which answers the old objection that a library upgrade could change hashes. This supersedes the earlier custom `Schema<T>` validator (D-011); the abandoned starter code is discarded. The offline verifier core (levels V0 to V4a and V5 for parser-free locators, section 13) stays dependency-free and validates bundle members with PSP, a restricted dialect of these schemas with an interpreter of about 250 lines, differentially tested against the pinned validator; its recompute module interprets the policy IR as data.

**Replay, defined narrowly.** For a fixed input, pinned code, policy and schema versions, and recorded effect cassettes, a replay yields the same normalised trace (defined in the M7 blueprint, component 14), the same final state hash and, for each ledger stream, the same leaf sequence and head leaf hash; never the same global ledger indices or tree root, which depend on case interleaving. It does not mean a live model is byte-identical or that sampling repeats (repeatability is a property of cassettes only); the trace records model id, prompt version, parameters, tool calls and response hashes.

**Cassettes have two layers.** The M7 effect record for `source.fetch` holds `{slot, reqHash, respHash, outcome}` with bodies in the content store; M1's transport cassette holds the network exchange (request key, hops, headers, body hash), and in replay M1 re-runs hashing, parsing, trust assignment and evidence building from it, the result having to equal the effect record's `respHash`. A replay miss is the fatal error `E_CASSETTE_MISS`, with a hint to run live. There is one blob store behind `StorePort`, addressed by `RawHash` and partitioned by confidentiality class, with tombstones; the erasure service (section 13) is its only deleter.

**Write-ahead ordering.** `LedgerPort` offers `append` (durable), `published` (explicit) and `streamHead`. Leaves that must be published before an irreversible effect (`approval.granted`, `capability.issued`, `action.intent`) go through `appendBefore`; result leaves (`action.executed`, `action.confirmed`) may lag with a write-ahead-log backfill that preserves order, so "no `action.executed` without a prior `action.intent` and `capability.issued`" holds by `walSeq` order.

**Runtime bake-off (D-009, revised by D-011).** Run before the substrate is built at full scale, as described in section 19a.

**Simulation harness.** Seeded fault injection at the ports (FoundationDB and TigerBeetle method), failures print `seed + commit`, replay corpus as a CI gate (13 §5).

Acceptance: the eight tests of `prior-art/06` §9.12 (replay equality, crash safety, budget soundness, order independence, gate integrity, failure drills, trace completeness, comparison), executable as T1 to T8 in the M7 blueprint with broken drivers that must each fail exactly the intended test. The write-ahead log, the harness and the broken drivers are built with the cassettes (build-order step 3), before bake-off 1. The budget guarantee is conditional (section 9a).

## 5. M8 Security layer (cross-cutting; `prior-art/10`)

**Trust levels (integrity axis), a lattice and not a number.** Four levels from most to least trusted: `SOR` (a system of record read under an authenticated identity, for a field whose last write did not derive from `UNTRUSTED` input), `VERIFIED` (a tool-obtained value from an authoritative channel for an attribute that channel's authority covers, or a typed attestation event, see below), `EXTRACTED` (a model extraction from a `SOR` or `VERIFIED` source), `UNTRUSTED` (external text as received, anything authored by the counterparty, and a model extraction from any `UNTRUSTED` input). **Human confirmation is not a level:** a person clicking confirm on an `UNTRUSTED` value does not make it `SOR` (that is the social-engineering path, ASI09). `combine(a, b)` returns the less trusted level (the lattice meet), so `combine(VERIFIED, UNTRUSTED) = UNTRUSTED`; a derived value takes `combine` over every input it depends on, with one stated exception: a **fused** value (a reconciled fact) takes the meet over its *supporting* inputs, the winning groups whose content determines the value. Defeated, ineligible and suspect inputs are recorded as `defeats`, hashed into the fact, and affect only cell status, conflict records and gaps, which are restrict-only; so an attacker-supplied conflicting assertion cannot downgrade a `SOR` value (an availability attack), and a fused value is never trusted above any input that supports it. Deterministic code preserves trust and cannot create it; whether a value is `raw` or `derived` is a separate attribute. Source authority (per source and attribute), `authorship` (`counterparty | third_party | authority`) and factual support (supported, contradicted, unresolved) are further independent attributes; none substitutes for another. A table in each pack assigns a level to every (source, attribute, producer) triple, and a permission matrix (`permission-matrix.json`, data hashed into the policy bundle) decides each action class from the full label (trust, authorship, producer) and not from trust alone: a counterparty-authored registry field can be `VERIFIED` and still may not satisfy a manifest item. M5a's interpreter looks each rule atom up in that matrix and carries no numeric level, so the offline recompute re-interprets the same data; both tables are test inputs. Assertions carry `authorship`, `producer` and `tier`, and a fused fact carries the label of each supporting group (`supportLabels`), so a fused value is decided by those labels: any denial denies, and a `corroborate` result permits only when at least `corroborationMin` independent origins (a pack parameter, default 1) whose labels allow the class support the same value. `featurize` computes both, so the recompute re-derives them from recorded facts. (This corrects v2, where "min" over numeric tier labels read the wrong way round.)

A level changes only by `combine` or by a **verification event**: a tool re-fetches from an authoritative channel and checks, or a typed attestation records evidence bound to an exact value. A verification event creates a new `VERIFIED` fact and never promotes the old one.

The risk is propagation, not algebra, so the main tests are propagation tests: every `Fact` constructor requires its parents; a mutation that drops a supporting parent must fail a test, and a mutation that drops a defeated parent from `defeats` fails a completeness check (every conflicting assertion that `fuse` considered must appear there); a taint-tracking run over the hero cases asserts that no derived fact has a level above any supporting parent. Property tests of `combine` (commutative, associative, idempotent, never above an input, `UNTRUSTED` absorbing) are kept but are weak on a four-element chain and are not offered as evidence of safety. A second, join-propagating **confidentiality** lattice uses one vocabulary (`public`, `case-visible`, `internal-sensitive`, with `personal` as a flag): a derived value takes the join, and a test asserts that no value above its declared egress class leaves, except at declared disclosure parameters where only the minimised form leaves (an identifier such as an LEI or CIK when one exists, the name only when none does; a public lookup necessarily discloses the relationship). Model prompts are an egress sink capped by the model profile, and precedent views cross cases only through a whitelist projection with a canary test. The deployment selects a `disclosureProfile` (`public_lookup`, `identifier_only`, `offline`). Foreign data classes (the Meterless documents use `public`, `internal`, `confidential`, `restricted`) map to this vocabulary through `dataClassMap.json`, owned by M12; an unmapped class maps to `internal-sensitive`.

**Write-path provenance and attestation.** Writes to the vendor master and other systems of record are events with provenance: the silo contract carries `last_write_*`, `change_request_id`, `request_origin` and `editor_sub` columns (planted by the generator, section 16), a write from `vendor_portal`, `vendor_email` or an unknown origin is `UNTRUSTED`, and a missing column makes the field `UNTRUSTED`. A field whose last write derived from vendor-submitted or otherwise `UNTRUSTED` input is `UNTRUSTED` until an **attestation** exists: a typed out-of-band step (for example a callback to a number held on file, never one supplied in the request) whose output is a signed attestation bound to the exact account value, channel, caller identity and time, which creates a `VERIFIED` fact; M8 designs and tests the step and M13 hosts it as an attestation task. "Days since bank-detail change" and "changed by requester" are policy inputs with their own lineage.

**Role-typed release fields.** The payee identifier is `SOR`. Account and amount accept `SOR` only, or a `VERIFIED` fact produced by an account-verification channel or an attestation (no public authoritative source for bank-account ownership exists, 15 §4, so the demo simulates and labels one). The payable amount is authored by the vendor upstream, so the release ceiling is `min(invoice amount, PO remaining)` with `PO remaining` internally originated. Registries corroborate and never assign a release field. The account is compared only as `accountCommit`, a tenant-salted commitment computed by one function shared by the silo mask, the binding and the broker's recomputation. The release is one `ExecutionContract` with capability `act/po.release` and a `ReleaseBinding`, which the broker verifies in one fixed order before any action (section 14a): M12 owns the container and its verify and consume rules, M8 the binding contents and minting policy, M13 the broker, the nonce store and execution-time re-validation. Injected text cannot become a payee by construction, **except through two named residual paths**: a compromised or socially engineered write to the system of record, and social engineering of the attestation step itself.

**Required positive evidence: release is a conjunction, not the absence of objections.** A release requires a manifest of release-enabling facts, each from a `SOR` or `VERIFIED` channel; the absence of a required document-derived fact is a **hold**, not a no-op. Every hold-relevant signal must be derivable from a channel the counterparty does not author, or be **double-extracted** (a deterministic parser and a model, disagreement being a conflict item) with schema-completeness checks (a certificate record with no expiry field holds).

**Restrict-only gate.** `EXTRACTED` and `UNTRUSTED` content and every model output may add a hold, an escalation or a check; none may add permission or change a release field. The judge's choice is restricted further in section 10. The guarantee, stated precisely: no injection can add permission or change a release field. An injection can still try to **suppress** a document-derived restriction (make the extractor omit or mis-type a hold-relevant fact); that residual is bounded by the policy requiring corroboration for release-enabling facts and is measured by attack family `SEC-I18` with the oracle "the decision equals the decision on the clean benign twin", reporting the extractor steer rate with an upper bound as well as the unauthorised-release rate. Two axes are reported separately: instruction injection versus authored deception (a well-formed lie such as a changed bank account is defeated by attestation or independent-source corroboration, not by labels), plus the availability cost of safety (forced escalations per vendor, with a rate limit so that escalation cannot be used as a denial-of-service lever).

**Compartments and their enforcement.** Privileged components run in one process and accept only labelled typed values built by a module-private class with a runtime registry (TypeScript brands are erased and forgeable with `as`; an AST test and forged-object rejection tests enforce it). The document extractor and parser run as a separate restricted child process with timeouts and memory caps. Isolation is defence in depth and the claim follows the backend that ran: process permission flags (no child process, addons, worker or WASI) are always set; network denial comes from an OS or container backend or from Node 25 or later, which has a network permission flag (the Node 22 line has none, per the blueprints' reading of its documentation; NOT VERIFIED beyond that reading); M8 probes the backend in the order `os-netns`, `node-net-permission`, `node-flags-only` and records which ran, so on a judge machine without a container the report says `node-flags-only` and no network-denial claim is made. The Node permission model is a seat belt by its own documentation, not a defence against an exploited parser, and M1's in-process SAMPLE_MODE network guard is a second, weaker control that never counts as the compartment claim. The extractor child reaches a model only through one parent-side forwarder of a single request type (M8 K2). Web query construction is deterministic; an LLM may only propose a `Proposal<DiscoveryQuery>` validated by M8's **closed grammar** and executed by a typed channel, only if M14's ablation shows a gain, and no role supplies a URL.

**Network funnel.** There is one network port, `ctx.net`, implemented in M8's `ports/egress.ts`, the only module that imports network libraries. M1 decides how to fetch (per-channel allowlist, SSRF pinning, size caps, rate limits, retries; redirects are off, each `send` is one exchange, M1 loops over hops and re-checks each against the allowed hosts, and passes a connect-time address policy that M8 enforces after name resolution); M8 decides whether the labelled content may leave, against one `egress-manifest.json` schema populated from the source metadata, the model profile and the Zetaris configuration.

**Quarantined extractor.** Closed output schema, quote spans checked by code, no free text out (10 §A4). There is one extraction schema: M8 owns the closed field kinds and the validator, M2 the parsers and value grounding, M14 the prompt. **Hidden-text defence** in M2. **Pinned surfaces:** prompts, tool descriptions and schemas are hash-pinned; a changed MCP tool list fails closed (12 §10).

**Evidence ladder (not a proof).** The structural claims are tested with an adversary-controlled model port, exhaustive small-scope enumeration of the policy table, mutation testing of the gate and label-unforgeability checks. These are statements about the policy table at a stated scope, not about the whole system, and every result prints that scope. Detectors and guard models are telemetry that force escalation, never the gate (10 §F2).

**Residual threats carried into the threat model.** Kill switch (it stops effects that have not crossed the send boundary; after it the broker reconciles and may propose a reversal); supply chain beyond hash pins (dependency compromise, lockfile review); timing and volume side channels of public lookups; approver authentication and identity binding in the approval UI (segregation-of-duties invariants depend on it); counterparty identity disclosed to GLEIF, SEC, CourtListener and any third party through typed queries (classified `internal-sensitive` in the egress manifest, with the note that lookups disclose relationships); custody of the release-capability signing key (one signer process and key registry with the roles ledger, decision, capability, attestation, control, erasure and run, section 13, or the exposure is stated); time-of-check to time-of-use between approval and execution (section 14a). Test ids come from `prior-art/10 §F4` and are listed in the module acceptance index (section 21b).

Threat model mapped to the OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026 (ASI01 to ASI10, read from the official PDF) with control and test id in `10 §F4`. We claim coverage mapping, never compliance. Live side effects stay disabled in the demo: the action path creates a simulated task or a human-review item unless real execution is explicitly configured and approved.

**Risk-lexeme sentinel and windowed extraction (for SEC-I18).** Extraction over long documents uses bounded sliding windows, and a deterministic multi-pattern scan (for example Aho-Corasick over a versioned lexicon of hold-relevant terms such as suspension, expiry, revoked, lien, sanction, default) runs over the whole visible text layer independently of the model. A lexeme present in the document with no corresponding extracted fact produces a restrict-only finding and a hold; this makes suppression by an injected instruction on one page visible on another. It is telemetry-grade (it cannot prove absence) and sits beside required-positive-evidence and double extraction, not instead of them.

## 6. M1 Source Fabric (`prior-art/02`)

One contract per source: metadata (licence, terms URL, allowed origins, rate budget, size and time limits), ordered backends, a real canary probe, `fetch` returning `Evidence` or a typed failure, content-addressed cache, strict cassette replay, and a doctor report with exit codes and structured credential redaction (including pre-signed URL parameters, a gap in Agent Reach).

**Authority is per attribute.** Identity and LEI: GLEIF where an LEI exists, otherwise the jurisdiction's official registry (no LEI does not mean invalid). Securities disclosures: SEC EDGAR for entities within its coverage. Sanctions: the official lists relevant to the jurisdiction; a non-match is never "clear" and the record keeps method, list version, retrieval time and coverage. Ownership and control: a current authoritative registry or filed disclosure with exact entity linkage; Wikidata discovers aliases only. Litigation: an official court source where feasible; CourtListener is a US search aid. Delivery, payment history, factory audits: internal systems or labelled synthetic fixtures. News and website signals: secondary, with publication time. Agent Reach-style channels serve secondary web discovery and never decide identity, sanctions, certification, ownership or operational safety. Source-type vocabulary seeds from the Beneficial Ownership Data Standard (07 §3.3). **Authorship and self-declaration.** Registries mostly record self-declared filings (LEI issuance rests on information supplied by third parties; company registers are mostly self-declared), so a counterparty-authored name, remark or other-names field arrives through an authoritative channel. Each attribute therefore carries `authorship` (`counterparty | third_party | authority`) and a `self_declared` authority tier; registry free-text fields are always `UNTRUSTED` (this includes authority-authored remarks such as OFAC's, which is acceptable because sanctions evidence only restricts); trust is assigned per (source, attribute), not per source. An **account-verification channel** (bank-account ownership or callback attestation) is added to the channel list and the gold set; for the demo it is simulated and labelled.

**Evidence record** (immutable; type in the shared conventions): evidence id (content-derived and including the retrieval time of the network exchange, so replay reproduces ids), channel id, retrieval time and effective date, exact locator (URL or system and record id plus page, offset in code points and UTF-8 bytes, or field; section 7), `bodyHash` (a `RawHash`, or a salted `Commitment` for anything above `public`; the same rule applies to `docSha`, `textLayerHash` and `spanSha`, so a plain digest of a non-public document, layer or quote never appears), `parsedHash` (a `DocHash`), parser version, authority and coverage notes, licence status, `synthetic`, `use` (`decision` or `discovery`; discovery evidence such as Jina can never be cited by a decision-bearing claim), raw and normalised value side by side, tier, and trust and confidentiality labels. The claim ids an item supports or contradicts live in a derived `EvidenceLinks` index owned by M4, not in the immutable record; erasure and replay misses are typed fetch errors (`erased`, `cassette_miss`), not fetch states. Raw body, parsed records, extracted facts and claims stay separate layers. Provenance is kept in separate dimensions that are never conflated: authenticity (who published it: registry authority, TLS origin, document signature validity), retrieval (when and how we obtained it), integrity (hash of the stored bytes), authority (per-attribute tier), temporal validity, and factual support. A hash proves that the stored bytes equal the recorded bytes; it proves nothing about who published them or whether they are true. W3C PROV-O is the reference vocabulary for export; internally we keep our richer record because PROV-O cannot express conflict or confidence (07 §4.2).

**Fetch states, never collapsed:** `ok`, `partial`, `not_found`, `not_checked`, `not_applicable`, `unavailable`, `blocked_by_policy`, `rate_limited`, `stale`.

**Wired sources observed 2026-10-13:** GLEIF LEI API, SEC EDGAR `data.sec.gov` (declared User-Agent required), OFAC SDN export (redirect to a signed URL; the 29 MB figure refers to `SDN.XML`, and the list version comes from the XML header plus a content hash because three version signals disagree), Wikidata, CourtListener search, Jina Reader (discovery only, never decision evidence: a third-party renderer stores its own transformation of the page and sees the target URL; any evidence used in a decision comes from a raw fetch), RSS. **Rights gate:** no source body is committed as a cassette or fixture until its reuse terms are verified. GLEIF and Wikidata are CC0; SEC, OFAC and CourtListener reuse terms are **NOT VERIFIED** and stay uncommitted (hashes and metadata only). OpenSanctions yente and nomenklatura are MIT-licensed code, but OpenSanctions data is CC BY-NC 4.0 with paid commercial licences (opensanctions.org/licensing, fetched 2026-10-13; whether a hackathon or grant counts as non-commercial is not stated): its data is used only as a local evaluation baseline where those terms allow and is never shipped in the repository. Demo fixtures are synthetic and labelled; no fabricated adverse event is ever attached to a real company. Sanctions-list and registry coverage beyond OFAC (EU, UN, UK) is a design item pending licence reading (15 §4).

**Document intake (channel 11j).** The hero case starts from documents, so their path to `Evidence` is a channel with fixed labels, because assigning authorship and trust is a security decision (M8): (a) case attachments from the console or case intake, authorship `counterparty` unless the pack's submitter role declares otherwise, trust `UNTRUSTED`; (b) the internal document lake through `SiloPort`'s `documents` view, trust ceiling `EXTRACTED`, authorship from repository metadata; (c) web-fetched documents, `UNTRUSTED`, `third_party`. Each produces `Evidence` with a `RawHash` body hash, a rights class and size and type caps, and hands the blob reference to M2. M13 `submitCase` carries `attachments: {name, mediaType, size, bodyHash}[]` with the bytes delivered to the content store by hash; intake caps (count, size, type) are M13's and content checks are M2's.

**Silo contract and roles.** The contract (tables, columns, types, `conf`, `personal`, `last_write_*`, views, masks, roles) is a product artefact owned by M1 and hashed (`contractHash`); the generator reads it (section 16). `SiloRole` has five values, `agent`, `buyer`, `reviewer`, `approver` and `broker`, and is the data-access identity; a table maps each of M13's RBAC roles to exactly one of them. Bundle audiences and MCP scopes are different axes (section 13).

**Zetaris access paths.** REST is the primary live path; JDBC is a record-time transport and typing oracle for the differential test (the vendor JAR is not on Maven and needs a JDK); the read-only MCP client is the fallback (12 §10.1), and the judge path replays cassettes. All of it stays NOT VERIFIED until a trial account exists. The MCP client is treated as an untrusted server: tool surface pinned by hash, allowlisted in the Scout registry, results parsed into our own schemas, read-only tools only, no token passthrough, JSON-RPC exchange cassetted with the surface hash in the key, `input_required` becomes a human step. Tool names, URL, auth and protocol revision are **NOT VERIFIED** until a trial account exists.

Acceptance: the eleven contract-test groups in `prior-art/02` §6.8, plus per-source terms review recorded in `docs/sponsors/`; the M1 blueprint maps them to named test groups and adds hard gates (a 100% block rate on the hostile corpus with zero sockets, no secret-like value in any artefact, replay equality).

## 7. M2 Document Intelligence (`prior-art/14`)

The headline cases depend on contracts, audits and certificates, so locators must be right.

- **One owned, hashed visible-text layer.** Page, offset, quote and hash are defined over it. The layer is not Unicode-normalised. Offsets are Unicode code points, 0-based with an exclusive end, page-relative over the visible layer; a `textlayer` locator also carries UTF-8 `byteStart` and `byteEnd` into the same page text, so the offline verifier slices bytes with no Unicode logic, checks that the slice equals the quoted bytes and that the code-point count before `byteStart` equals `start`, and any disagreement is a FAIL (`V5.LOCATOR_UNIT_MISMATCH`). Span records and their ids belong to M2 (24 hex characters, including the page); models see per-run aliases rather than ids, and M4's span type is a view over them. Verification is at four levels, named `LV1` to `LV4`: offline slice check, extractor replay, a second independent extractor agrees, render plus OCR. A quote that crosses a page break is cited as ordered single-page fragments joined by `page_break` join hints: L0 checks the adjacency and the verifier checks each fragment (adjacency is not provable offline). Every M2 output is labelled from its document's `Evidence` and never above it.
- **The model points, the system quotes.** The extractor returns span aliases, which code maps to span ids; code fills `exact` text and hash; unknown aliases and ids are rejected by L0.
- **Per-glyph visibility from two channels** (PDF operators and rendered pixels). Evidence is issued only from visible text; hidden or white-on-white text is not discarded but becomes a typed risk signal that feeds policy. OCR layers are legitimately invisible, so an unconditional strip is wrong. The visibility filter excludes and signals and never rewrites the layer. On a scanned page the visible layer is our own OCR; the native invisible layer is compared against it and never used as evidence. Document findings feed `escalate`, never `reject`, and the absence of findings is never clearance (M5a receives `doc.risk.<code>` and `doc.complete.<docType>` as restrict-only inputs).
- **Revision diff for signed documents**: separate fields for signature present, valid, and content-unchanged since signing (closes the Shadow Attack gap). Both this and the per-glyph visibility check are of unverified feasibility in Node under our install-size and disk constraints (the operator list must be walked because text extraction returns no colour or clip, pixel rendering needs a renderer, signature validation needs CMS handling); they are part of bake-off 4 with stated fallbacks (a coarser text-layer-versus-OCR diff; signature presence reported without validity).
- **Parser chosen by measurement** on a locator-fidelity benchmark with by-construction ground truth (one document-corpus generator owned by M11, its typesetters chosen by a recorded spike, known glyph boxes); primary metric is the rate of silently wrong locators. Tier 1 Node extractors (pdf.js with `stopAtErrors` set to true, PDFium; `@embedpdf/pdfium` is a bake-off 4 candidate) are the judge-run default, so no Python runs at judge time; Tier 2 is Docling as an **offline pre-ingestion step** whose structure outputs are committed fixtures that serve as hints re-anchored into our layer and never as locators. PyMuPDF/MuPDF is AGPL and stays a test oracle only unless Prateek decides otherwise. Scanned documents go through a declared OCR path, never a silent fallback. We promise line-level highlights only where measured accuracy supports it.
- **Character-encoding attacks.** Glyph-shape remaps (custom encodings, ToUnicode tampering, ActualText) are covered by the render-and-OCR check; it is made mandatory for every span cited by a decision-bearing claim (a small, bounded set), with a divergence reported as a structural finding that escalates. Bidirectional controls and mixed-script confusables (Unicode UTS #39) are detected and reported as risk findings; the text layer is not rewritten to hide them, so locators and the verifier's slices stay exact.
- **PDF as an attack surface:** JavaScript, embedded files, hidden text, metadata; the parser is isolated and resource-limited and fuzzed with hostile files.

Acceptance: a cited page and line maps to the correct source text on the full corpus; silent-wrong rate reported with document-clustered intervals; decision-flip rate with visibility enforcement on versus off (the positive control, enforcement off, must be nonzero); the named tests are in the M2 blueprint (`DI-U`, `DI-V`, `DI-S`, `DI-M` families).

- **Document tree and cross-reference follower (adapted from PageIndex).** Intake builds a deterministic structural tree from the PDF outline and heading structure (`{nodeId, title, pageStart, pageEnd, spanIds[], crossRefs[]}`); a clause such as "subject to the liability cap in Schedule B" is followed through `crossRefs` to the target node's spans, and the traversal is traced. Model-written node summaries are navigation aids only, labelled `EXTRACTED`, never evidence; where no outline exists the tree falls back to layout headings and is marked low-confidence. Gate: a cross-reference retrieval test on planted contract clauses against a flat-chunk baseline.

## 8. M3 Reconcile (`prior-art/01`, `07`)

Pipeline: channel fetch -> evidence store -> extraction -> assertions -> link decisions -> conflict detection -> truth table.

- **Mention-level assertions, derived entities.** `Assertion { id, mentionId, attribute, raw, norm, source, originId, observedAt, validFrom?, validTo?, recordedAt, supersededAt?, extractor, parserVersion, evidenceId, trust, conf, personal, derivedFrom[], diag? }` (the shared type; the locator is reached through `evidenceId`). Entity ids are a derived view over judgement edges (FollowTheMoney/nomenklatura pattern, 07 §1.3). Un-merge then re-merge yields an identical truth table.
- **Three-region link decision** (link / possible / non-link) with an additive weight ledger in which each line is checkable, qualifier-only features, `cannot-link` edges that block clusters, and a transitivity audit. Cascade: authoritative ids, normalised exact, gated fuzzy (type and discriminator agreement; "Series A" versus "Series B" must not merge), then model matching confined to the possible band, with abstention. The select call only nominates; the recorded edge comes from a pairwise confirmation in both orders whose inputs are exactly the two mentions' assertions, which keeps edge locality testable (if the call budget forbids it, the inputs are the union of all shown candidates and the certificate says `locality: "set"`). A failed or abstaining match leaves the pair `possible`; a model judgement never clears a screening candidate and never alone satisfies a required positive-evidence entry (`applyMatcher` takes a direction, `screening` or `corroboration`). Model self-explanations are not evidence.
- **Declared lineage for independence.** An origin DAG (mirrors, aggregators) is data; independent support is counted over origins; same-origin duplicates count once. Pages of unknown origin default to one origin per registrable domain and unknown mirrors share one origin; the default is reported, and inference about unknown origins may only lower independence counts. Automatic copy detection is advisory only (Li et al. 2013 found misfires, 07 §2.3).
- **Time-decayed comparison** to separate "update over time" from "different entity".
- **Provenance expressions on every derived cell** (Green et al. semiring provenance, 07 §4.1): one stored object yields exact retraction, independent-source counts, best authority tier and the necessity table.
- **Conflict taxonomy as code:** same-name-different-entity, update over time, source disagreement, extraction error. Extraction-versus-source-error hypotheses are ordered so a parse error is not reported as a source conflict.
- **Resolution** is a pure function of dates, authority tiers, trust eligibility, authorship, a freshness guard and declared per-attribute cardinality (the rank vector of M3 component 8). **Repair semantics** are the normative definition of `unresolved`, a data-conflict status on a truth-table cell; minimal flips become minimum hitting sets, cross-checked by clingo offline (07 §6.1). A truth-table cell has five statuses, `resolved`, `unresolved`, `silent`, `not_checked` and `unavailable`, shared with M5 and M10; `silent` means every required channel was checked and nothing says, and no projection turns it into `not_checked`. Cells are keyed by the anchor mention (`cell:<anchorMention>/<attribute>`, the least mention id of the entity in the base snapshot) because entity ids are membership-derived and change with the present evidence. A fact's trust is the meet over its supporting groups only, and what it defeated is recorded (section 5).
- **Evidence-mass triple** (support, oppose, silent) over independent origins with rule-derived ordinal tags; counts merge by set union and are not additive. No Dempster rule, no learned reliability weights in the path (kept as an offline audit report, 07 §10).
- **Identity-hypothesis robustness.** For every `possible` link, enumerate the admissible identity clusterings under cannot-link constraints, run Reconcile and the rule engine under each, and report `stable` or `identity_dependent` with the minimal set of link judgements whose reversal changes the verdict. `identity_dependent` forces escalation. The enumeration is bounded: it is pruned by the static cone (only links touching verdict-relevant attributes), capped, and returns `identity_dependent` conservatively with `completeness: partial` when the cap is hit; the forced-escalation rate is reported against the false-merge benefit, and an ablation turns the feature off. (Research contribution, 07 §11.1.)
- **Round-table of source advocates** is an experimental arm, not the default (12 §8). Unresolved stays unresolved and escalates.

Acceptance: conflict precision and recall by type; entity false-merge rate (zero at the product threshold on planted near-duplicates, with interval); idempotent re-run gives an empty diff, a mutated re-run the expected diff; for monotone support, provenance-expression retraction equals recomputation on every cell, and for a defeated single-valued cell a cell-local re-fuse equals recomputation; planted copy chains never raise independent support above one; the named tests are `RC-1.1` to `RC-16.6` in the M3 blueprint.

## 9. M4 Explain (`prior-art/04`, `08`)

Claims form a **typed ledger**; prose is rendered from it, so every sentence maps back.

- **L0 deterministic, offline:** quote-in-source per cited item (exact, normalised, ordered ellipsis, near, misattributed, absent, `outside_span`, `quote_too_short`, `exact_cross_page` for ordered per-page fragments; `near` is a warning and never a pass), source hash, numeric and date consistency, decomposition drift guard. `fabricated_quote` ends a claim without a model call.
- **L1 decomposition** into atomic self-contained claims, in two arms. The default is **extractive**: each atom is an ordered list of spans of the parent sentence plus entity handles, so atom text cannot contain a word the sentence did not, and self-containment is entity-handle substitution. The control arm is generative SAFE-style decomposition with a rewrite pass. Relevance filter (unknown relevance is kept and reported), drift, coverage and atomicity checks and dedupe apply to both arms; sentence fallback is labelled and never mixed into LLM-grade metrics.
- **L2 entailment** with `entailed | partial | neutral | contradicted` (silence and opposition separated), a required deciding span that L0 re-checks, a dual-prompt judge, a different model family from the producer where possible, and an offline lexical gate. Judge over-commit rate is measured. A non-LLM second voter backs high-stakes classes. A label resting only on counterparty-authored support is `entailed` only in reported form (`self_declared_only`), and a label chain that rests on `UNTRUSTED` content cannot permit (M5a derives `claim.<id>.canPermit` itself from the chain label through the permission matrix; M4's flag is a display copy).
- **L3 advocate / skeptic**, narrow: symmetric tool-using searchers over the stored corpus, outputs admitted only as quotes L0 verifies, run only on claims routed by the sensitivity router (claims whose removal can change the verdict, computed exactly because the policy is code). Arbiter: a deterministic rule over verified evidence settles most cases; a panel of different-family models handles the residue and `contested` is never forced to a vote.
- **Exact causal layer (L4a), the core novelty (08 §1).** A **frozen ledger snapshot** holds evidence items E (each passed L0), judged edges (link judgements and L2 labels, each with its cited spans or candidate pair), gaps, a policy version and an as-of date. `V(S)` for S ⊆ E is the pure composition reconcile, label aggregation, policy, evaluated with only the evidence in S: edges whose cited evidence is absent are dropped and the remaining edges are taken as recorded. `V` has one owner and one interface: M4's `Evaluator` composes `V = phase2 ∘ phase1`, where `phase1` is M3's `fuse` after M4's label aggregation and M5a's `featurize`, and `phase2` is M5a's `evaluate`. M3 adapts `fuse` and `statusUnder` into `keys`, `deps` and `phase1`; M5a adapts `coneOf` and `fieldDeps` into `reads(projection)` and maps its derivation to M4's content-addressed `DerivationTrace`; `vSpecHash`, the hash of the three halves' versions and the projection, is printed in every certificate. Fuzzy link scores never enter `V`, only recorded link judgements do, and the verifier's recompute module interprets exactly `phase1` and `phase2` as data. **Edge locality** makes that meaningful: each judgement's input is exactly its cited spans or candidate pair, enforced by re-judging with non-cited evidence removed and checking the label is unchanged; without locality, V(S ∖ R) would mean only what the frozen edges imply. `v* = V(E)`. **Robustly sufficient evidence** (RS): S such that for all T ⊆ E ∖ S, V(S ∪ T) = v*. **Minimal robustly sufficient evidence** (MRS): a subset-minimal RS set (not the smallest set that alone yields the verdict; V is not monotone, so that weaker notion is rejected). **Minimal flip** (MF): a subset-minimal R ⊆ E with V(E ∖ R) ≠ v*. S is RS iff it intersects every flip, so MRS and MF are minimal-hitting-set duals (Reiter 1987, Ignatiev et al. 2019); if no flip exists the verdict does not depend on the evidence, which is reported as a defect and not as a strong explanation. They are computed exactly by re-running the pure function with static-cone pruning, attribute decomposition and an implicit-hitting-set loop, with `completeness: partial` when a budget is hit, never silent truncation. Halpern-Pearl causes and responsibility on the derivation DAG are an optional layer (section 21a), justified only by graded responsibility and explicit witnesses, since the minimal-evidence classes already separate overdetermined evidence (two registries confirming one fact) from decorative evidence, which leave-one-out cannot. Edits beyond removal (correct a value, supply an unchecked source) give the minimal change that flips the verdict and a **decision-relevant evidence-gap analysis**: whether any value an unchecked source could return would change the verdict under policy P (this is not an expected value of information). Requires the evaluator to emit a derivation trace.
- **Judged-edge layer (L4b):** the existing leave-one-out, distractor and empty-evidence probes, confined to the L2 labels, plus a **robustness radius**: the minimum number of judge errors that flips the verdict, with per-label error rates calibrated from a small gold set.
- **Argumentation semantics (optional layer, gated by an ablation).** `unresolved` is the reconcile data-conflict status defined by repair semantics in section 8; `contested` is a claim-level status in the ledger. Dung semantics over a framework built mechanically from verified evidence (no LLM-written arguments) may formalise `contested` and supply dispute-tree certificates, but only if a stated mapping to the repair-semantics statuses is shown to agree on the gold set and an ablation shows the layer changes an output; otherwise `contested` keeps a simple rule-based definition and the layer stays on the roadmap.
- **Certificates.** Each flip, sufficiency and argument result ships a certificate the offline verifier re-checks. A certificate's own outcome is `VALID`, `INVALID` or `INCONCLUSIVE`; the verifier's verdict on it is a separate vocabulary (`PASS | FAIL | INDETERMINATE | NOT_APPLICABLE`): a re-check that agrees with the producer's outcome is PASS even when that outcome is INVALID, a disagreement is FAIL, and budget exhaustion is INDETERMINATE. Counterfactual wording is fixed: "under this fixed case snapshot and policy version, removing evidence E changes the computed verdict from A to B"; it is a sensitivity test, not a claim about real-world causation.
- **Six checks kept separate** in code, metrics and UI: citation integrity, quote fidelity, attribution and entailment, factuality across sources, conflict detection, decision correctness. Agreement among model agents is never a substitute for evidence.
- **Gap ledger** caps verdict confidence, but only for gaps that are relevant or of unknown relevance, not for gaps proven irrelevant within the declared domain. Confidence is an ordinal tag derived by rule; probabilities are shown only after calibration earns them.

Acceptance: exactness against brute force on random ledgers to 14 items; differential run against an SMT encoding of the policy function (reconcile is not encoded) and an independent second implementation of V over a reduced reconcile (brute force and the enumerator share V, so they validate the enumerator and not V); the ledger-size distribution of the hero cases and oracle-call counts are published; planted MSE and MF known by construction; mutation tests (delete, correct, add a contradiction) matching predicted change; tamper tests the verifier must reject; metrics of `prior-art/04` §10 (citation recall and precision, unsupported-claim rate, contradiction-detection recall with false-contradiction rates, all with cluster-bootstrap intervals); evidence-corruption attacks; fabricated-sentence-with-real-URL test; the `Evaluator` obligations 1 to 9 as named tests (M4 blueprint, C8); a normative `l0-spec` with vectors published before the verifier's `textlayer` work.

## 9a. Guarantee boundaries (what each check proves and does not)

Every exact or "verified" statement in this spec is relative to a stated frame. Reports and certificates list what was checked and what is assumed; none is a universal proof that a decision is true or wise.

| Dimension | What is established | What is not established |
|---|---|---|
| Integrity | stored bytes, bundle members and ledger entries are unchanged since recording (hashes, Merkle checkpoints), and verified against pinned keys when the relying party supplies them; a bundle re-signed with a key the relying party does not pin fails (`V2.SIGNER_NOT_PINNED`) | who published them, whether they are accurate, whether the source was compromised; without relying-party pins the verdict is INDETERMINATE (self-supplied trust); rights-gated PDF evidence reaches V5 offline only through the stored text layer |

Rates: the measured silent-wrong locator rate with its document-clustered upper bound, per-technique hidden-text recall and benign false-quarantine rate, on the PLD-1 test split | that the original PDF says it in any viewer other than the PDFium rendering used; documents of types, producers, scripts or layouts outside PLD-1; visual forgery (edited images, fabricated seals) and handwriting; shape or encoding remaps on pages where the OCR cross-check did not run; OCR text beyond its measured accuracy; whether a signer is who they claim; the truth of the content |

## 10. M5 Decide: M5a policy core and M5b decision pipeline (`prior-art/05`, `09`)

CoALA made concrete: working (per-run typed state), episodic (case records), semantic (versioned facts with tiers), procedural (policy-as-code; never an LLM-rewritten prompt).

Pipeline: normalise case -> **rule engine** -> precedent retrieval (M6) -> judgement confined to the allowed set -> hard and soft critic -> bounded reflexion -> selective-release gate -> autonomy gate -> decision record -> approval item (the pipeline ends at `awaiting_approval`; the approval itself is a case state, section 14a). M5a (policy IR, interpreter, feature programs, `evaluate`, `derive`, `coneOf`, `flipSearch`, `revalidate`) depends on nothing above M7 and M8; M5b (judge, critics, selective release, autonomy, approval item semantics) depends on M3, M4, M6 and M5a, which keeps the dependency graph acyclic.

- **Policy IR with two interpreters.** A typed intermediate representation of rules with a runtime interpreter (TypeScript) and an SMT encoding (Z3, test and CI time). Invariants I1 to I9 (for example "no release above the cap without CRO approval", "approval never granted to the requester") are checked first by exhaustive cell enumeration against the real TypeScript interpreter, within the enumerable fragment (single-field atoms and the unit-coefficient two-field closure; anything outside it is reported `scope: partial`), with no solver in the loop, and second by Z3 proofs over the encoded rules with a differentially tested encoder; the evidence concerns the encoded rules, and derived-feature functions (days since change, similarity thresholds, date windows, ratios, set membership) either sit inside the verified surface with their own conformance tests or are treated as `SOR` inputs. A proof bundle is committed. Rule atoms name an `ActionClass` and are decided against the permission matrix by the full label, never by a numeric level. The feature registry also carries restrict-only inputs from other modules (`doc.risk.<code>`, `doc.complete.<docType>`, `manifest.<item>.status`, `claim.<id>.canPermit`, `overlay.releaseBlock`, `target.reversible`, and the exposure rows); a missing or unknown value can only hold. The IR is provisional pending bake-off 2, whose gating criterion is that the bundle format be interpretable by a dependency-free verifier-side evaluator (section 19a). Cedar's WASM is not used as "the verified engine" (no SMT analysis in the WASM build); Cedar SymCC is a CI cross-check for the stateless fragment only (09 §9). Stateful policies (cumulative exposure with an inductive invariant), obligations, dispensations and explicit rule precedence are in the IR. Stack layers (jurisdiction, then company) can only restrict, so the IR cannot express a law that forces a more permissive behaviour (**LAWYER**).
- **Rule-change gate.** Every proposal is classified tightening or loosening by SMT, with tiered approvals, segregation-of-duties and liveness checks, and an ADR; `unknown` and timeout classify as loosening and the availability cost is reported. Configuration changes (TTLs, retention, ceilings, cadences, approver counts) are classified by a declared direction per parameter (`ParamDecl`), an unclassifiable change is loosening and needs two approvers, and the governance roster and tiers are a second policy bundle evaluated by the same interpreter, so invariants I4 and I5 cover them. Proof-carrying rule changes bundle this with counterfactual evaluation (09 §15.2).
- **Judgement** receives the case, the allowed set `A`, fired rule ids and precedents as delimited data, and cites handles that code maps back to real ids, so a fabricated id is inexpressible. With recommended action `r` and the permissiveness order `release ≻ release_with_approval ≻ escalate ≻ hold ≻ reject` (a total order held as data in the policy IR, asserted by a test and used by the verifier), a selection `s ⪯ r` is accepted autonomously because it confirms or restricts; `s ≻ r` with `s ∈ A` becomes a `judge_loosening` approval item and nothing executes on the model's say-so; `s ∉ A` is rejected. The validator tests `s ∈ A` for every selection, and the policy IR asserts that `A` is downward closed in the permissiveness order. If the judge fails at the end of its retries the selection is `r`, the record says `judgeFailed`, and the selective-release gate treats the case as outside the certified region. The validator (checks `JV1` to `JV11`) rejects, never repairs; its precedent checks `JV7` to `JV9` are the outputs of M6's `validateJudgement` (`JV5` only resolves handles), and M6's `PrecedentRetrieval` is the boundary type. If the allowed set has one member the model is not called and the trace says `judge_skipped: single_allowed_action`; the precedent retrieval still runs once per decision.
- **Selective release with a stated bound.** Learn-then-Test style calibration on the synthetic generator with an abstain-to-human region, validated on 1,000 independent generator draws, each split by vendor cluster (re-splitting one dataset gives dependent replicates and an invalid interval), plus an attacker-shift stratum; per-stratum auto-coverage, abstain rate and realised harmful-release rate reported, with the alpha values published beside a money-loss reading. The figure 45 is arithmetic for zero harmful events in one stratum (certifying 5% at 90% confidence); the conditional selective-risk validity in 09 §10.2 is our derivation and **NOT VERIFIED** until the simulation confirms it. The bound holds for the synthetic generator only and is never claimed for the real world. For money-moving actions `release` at `AL2` produces a one-approver item and `release_with_approval` a multi-approver item, so "auto-coverage" means fast-lane (single-confirm) coverage; the certified statement is conditional on the calibrated region, and the joint rate can only fall when more cases abstain. Reversibility comes from `TargetManifest.reversal` (M13), not from a model. The 1,000 independent draws support the rule-brain claim; any model-scored claim uses fewer draws and says so.
- **Counterfactual evaluation of proposed rules** from logged decisions, with an explicit `unevaluable` verdict where a deterministic logger has no propensities, effective sample size, clipped doubly robust estimates, high-confidence lower bound, sensitivity analysis and multiplicity control. **Governed audit sampling** randomises only inside the proved-safe action set to create logged propensities. Replay agreement with humans is imitation, not outcome, and is never presented as rule value. Tightening a rule is evaluable from logged decisions under a stated hold-cost assumption; loosening is `unevaluable` without exploration, so the claim "evaluate any rule" is not made.
- **Autonomy ladder** `AL0` observe, `AL1` propose, `AL2` act with confirm, `AL3` act; irreversible or high-impact actions capped at `AL2` by rule. Promotion is an approval item gated by an anytime-valid off-policy confidence sequence (a Wilson check is invalid under repeated looks); demotion is automatic on incident. **Approval items** come from one service: M13 owns storage, WebAuthn verification, step-up, expiry clocks and the API; M5 owns the item semantics as policy data (item types, slots, eligibility, what expiry means per type); M8 owns the identity primitives. M5b reaches the service only through M7's `ApprovalPort` and M13 depends on M5a's item types and eligibility function, so the packages stay acyclic. An "extend review" is a re-issue (a new request with the same binding digest, a fresh nonce and expiry, a bounded count) that invalidates earlier approvals; the approver's provisional read and evidence-opened events live in a session keyed by the binding digest, outside any hashed request. There are eight item types: action, memory write, rule proposal, ladder promotion, judge loosening, dispensation, roster change and emergency ratification. Default on timeout is deny, with three named clocks (`requestExpiresAt`, the approval window `W` across several approvals, and the capability TTL); expiry of a pending action item moves the case to `HELD` with disposition `expired_unapproved` and a review timer, and the only expiry that loosens is the lapse of an emergency ratification, bounded to 72 hours and shown. A person's approval of a memory write or an attestation records `approvedBy` or `attestedBy` and never changes a label (`SEC-W1`). Approval cards render from typed fields with ERP-versus-claim diffs; friction (open the evidence) appears only at the approval gate, with logged evidence-opened events.
- **Reflexion** triggers only on a deterministic failure or a human rejection, at most two retries, then escalation with the full trail. The soft LLM critic is advisory and never the sole trigger; the critic rubric gets one advisory revision, separate from deterministic-failure reflexion.
- **Segregation of duties** is data in the case: actor identities for requester, vendor-master editor and approver, plus a pattern view over past approvals (ACFE: collusion defeats four-eyes). Actors are bound to identity-provider subjects; shared-credential and alias detection is recorded as a gap, because the invariants assume distinct people.
- **Methodology scope.** Off-policy evaluation, governed audit sampling, ladder-promotion statistics and the selective-release bound all run on a simulator and an outcome process we author, so they validate methodology, not the value of any real rule; in a real deployment, randomising payment actions would also need a business and ethics decision.

Acceptance: the named test groups `T-IR`, `T-FE`, `T-EN`, `T-SMT`, `T-BO2`, `T-INV`, `T-GATE`, `T-JDG`, `T-PRE`, `T-SEL`, `T-OPE`, `T-LAD`, `T-APQ`, `T-RFX`, `T-REC` and `T-PIPE` of the M5 blueprint; ablation ladder (rules only; plus precedent; plus recency, kept only if the ladder measures a gain; plus outcome; plus rule-version awareness; plus counter-precedent; with and without critic) on a labelled decision set including planted rule changes; veto-fires counterfactual replay; proofs reproduce in CI; selective-release simulation; mutation suite of bad rule changes the gate must catch.

**Autonomy vocabulary aligned to the organizer workshop.** The autonomy ladder is chosen by context delta (how far the current case is from the closest verified precedent) and named as in the organizers' slides: AL0 Macro (fixed script, observe), AL1 Confirm (propose, a human acts), AL2 Loop with stops (act with confirmation and bounded loops), AL3 Free-run (act, still logged and reversible where possible). Irreversible or high-impact actions stay capped at AL2 by rule. The four named agentic pairings (Planner+Doer, Proposer+Critic, Context-Packer+Actor, Evidence-Weaver+Synthesizer) are tags on trace handoffs (module 07, module 14) so a reviewer sees them without reading code, and the critic's 0 to 3 by three rubric (pass at 7 of 9) is recorded per revision.

## 11. M6 Memory and Learning (`prior-art/05`, `11`; Meterless H-MEM, World Model, Markovian)

- **Episodes are immutable** in id, digest and trust label. Consolidation never deletes or edits a source; erasure replaces a body with a tombstone and a signed receipt (section 13), and its consequences are named states, not silent ones: replay `CassetteMiss` with reason `erased`, verifier `INDETERMINATE` (`V5.ERASED`), and `unevaluable` for precedent validity and retraction. Its only output type is a `RuleProposal` (or a derived summary view with `derivedFrom[]`) carrying a **certificate**: temporal-holdout replay with precision, recall and a Wilson lower bound per rule version, mutation tests that flip exactly where the rule text says, non-amplification of trust tier, and unchanged source hashes. The queue rejects, never repairs. A model proposes; deterministic code certifies; a human approves. The consolidation-harm curve (accuracy against number of dream cycles) is measured for unconstrained LLM consolidation versus our proposal-only pipeline (one 2026 preprint reports that LLM consolidation can end below no memory, 11 §2.2).
- **Trust-level algebra** (the lattice of section 5) with non-amplification and typed atoms (evidence, cue, claim). Permissive-direction precedents that are tainted or unproven are excluded from every set and listed under `excluded` (a tainted precedent that argues for permission is not a counter); restrictive-direction ones may be shown. Autonomy above `AL1` needs a precedent whose `precedentTrust`, the meet of the policy-relevant fact trust and the outcome-evidence trust, is `SOR` or `VERIFIED`. Learning is restrict-only too: a proposal with unproven support may not loosen. Bounded occupancy at retrieval replaces additive trust penalties.
- **Decision-dependency graph with exact cascading retraction.** Each decision stores the ids it used. Retracting a fact, a precedent or a rule version recomputes the deterministic stage of every affected decision exactly and reports which change allowed-action set, which only change judgement input (re-judge queue), and which are unaffected. Poison removal, rule rollback and bad-source discovery share one mechanism. Exactness holds for the policy and bounded-state stages, not for the model judgement, and for the first order only: a decision that used an affected decision as a precedent is exact only when the predecessor's counterfactual action does not depend on the judge, otherwise it is flagged `second_order` and never called unaffected; erased snapshots are `unevaluable`.
- **Outcome-weighted, rule-version-aware precedent retrieval with mandatory counter-precedents.** Validity is recomputed by re-running the current rule engine on the stored case snapshot and marking `stale_rule_diff`; the score is the H-MEM spec score times fixed published factors for outcome and version (no learning); a counter-query returns at least one opposite-action or reversed-outcome precedent when one exists, and the judge must cite or dismiss it. Outcomes enter ranking only through the published factor; the engine's `feedback()` is reserved for authenticated reviewer verdicts, and recency, if kept, uses `decidedAt` and only if the precedent ladder measures a gain. Survivorship correction uses logged propensities from governed audit sampling where an outcome is observable; without them, or when the known share falls below its minimum, the system says "weakly informed". For holds and rejects the outcome is `censored` permanently absent adjudication, so that state is not fixable by sampling. The judge must cite or dismiss every returned counter-precedent with one of four mechanically checked dismissals (`rule_changed`, `fact_differs:<featureKey>`, `outcome_unverified`, `tainted`). Precedent views cross cases only through a whitelist projection with a canary test. Outcome labels never come from the deciding model.
- **Bitemporal facts** with as-of replay and in-query temporal filtering; supersede, never delete.
- **Markovian carryover carries fact ids, not facts**, validated against the World Model before each chunk, with a `carryover-overflow` state. Chunked reasoning is used only inside one deep case (a long ownership chain), never across counterparties: carryover that crosses independent vendors would leak facts between them, so a batch is a fan-out of independent per-case runs plus a code-level aggregate over typed results (for example cumulative exposure), and a contamination test (vendor B run after vendor A in one chain must not cite A's facts) guards the boundary. A deterministic **ratify** step checks that every finding id the decision needs is present in the final carryover and re-reads each from the World Model, so a dropped sanctions hit is caught in code and not by another model call. Decision-time precedent retrieval is a pure function of a pinned snapshot (the reference H-MEM mutates ranking on every read, so retrieval here is side-effect free and the pin is recorded in the trace); we do not claim token savings for short cases and report measured numbers, including where chunking loses.
- **Sleep-time precompute** is a keyed cache of derived, re-verified artefacts that never holds a decision.
- **Poisoning defences** run through the real write path: MINJA and AgentPoison style attacks adapted to documents and agent-authored memory; content filters are signals, never gates.

- **Progressive-disclosure memory interface (adapted from claude-mem).** Memory is exposed to internal agents and to MCP clients in three layers so prompts stay small and a case sees only what it selected: `search` returns compact stubs (id, title, layer, outcome, rule version, score), `timeline` returns the bitemporal window and causal neighbours of one anchor, `getObservations` returns full payloads only for chosen ids, through the whitelist projection. Gate: token and egress measurements against a full-dump baseline, and the canary test of the projection.
- **Entity-link candidate generation (adapted from HippoRAG).** Precedent candidates may also be generated by a bounded, deterministic walk (integer-arithmetic personalised ranking) over the bipartite graph of World Model entities and precedent episodes, seeded on the current case's entities, to surface cases that share a nominee, address or bank account; the similarity gate, outcome and rule-version factors and the counter-precedent rule then apply unchanged. It is a candidate source, not a replacement for retrieval, and ships only if an ablation shows it finds relevant precedents the feature similarity misses.
- **Certified playbooks (adapted from Hermes-style skill learning).** A successful investigation or re-plan may yield a reusable playbook template (for example which registry resolved a jurisdiction conflict fastest). Playbooks are `Proposal`s with the same temporal-holdout certificate as rule proposals, never silently overwritten, and affect routing only (they can add checks, never remove them).

## 13. M9 Audit and Bundle (`prior-art/13`)

No accepted standard for an AI decision audit bundle exists as of our searches (**re-search before claiming publicly**); 2026 drafts stop at "recorded and signed".

- **Decision bundle:** decision record, evidence bodies or hashes (rights-gated bodies are hash-only and verdict INDETERMINATE, never PASS), ledger chain, policy bundle and version, cassette manifest, certificates. The layout is content-addressed (`evidence/blobs/sha256/..` plus `evidence/index.json`); document-derived members (text layer, spans, findings, page rasters) are hashed derived roles under `evidence/derived/<docId>/`, and rasters and quads are display aids outside the verified chain. The cassette manifest carries the Merkle roots of both cassette layers. A bundle is a statement as of one checkpoint.
- **Ledger:** RFC 9162 Merkle history tree on C2SP tiles (tree values are `TreeHash`, `tlog:`), signed checkpoints, gapless per-case sequence numbers, declared checkpoint cadence, optional witness cosigning; anchoring with RFC 3161 and OpenTimestamps; key rotation as signed leaves; signing keys in a separate process; a single writer fenced by an epoch (`writer.epoch`, stale writers refused). There is one log per deployment and tenant (`<ns>/ledger/<deploymentId>/<tenantId>`; the judge path runs one tenant). The leaf format `LeafV1` and its registry are normative: streams `deploy | policy | memory | case:<id> | hold:<id> | erasure`, actor kinds `system | role | human | service` (a model is never an actor kind, because models write nothing and only propose), and registry names such as `action.intent`, `action.executed`, `action.confirmed`, `approval.granted`, `capability.issued` and `restore.epoch`, plus `approver.enrolled` and `approver.revoked` (approver credential public keys live in the ledger; enrolment is a two-person roster change after a flagged bootstrap), `writer.epoch`, `attestation.recorded`, the policy lifecycle names (`policy.proposed`, `policy.activated`, `policy.emergency_tightened`, `policy.lapsed`) and the autonomy names (`autonomy.promoted`, `autonomy.demoted`, `autonomy.frozen`); stage outputs are `stage.committed` and memory heads are `memory.head`. `append` is durable and publication is separate and mandatory before an irreversible effect (section 4). One signer process and one key registry serve the roles `ledger | decision | capability | attestation | control | erasure | run`; the `run` role signs only a contract without any `act/` capability and can never sign one, and there is no receipt or countersign role; the capability role signs only on the conjunction of an inclusion proof for every `approval.granted` leaf the binding lists, a WebAuthn assertion verified against an enrolled credential and bound to the body through the request's binding digest (one pure primitive shared with M13), and the `G-FRESH` guard (section 14a). We claim tamper-evidence under named assumptions only, never "tamper-proof" or a SLSA level. **Staged:** stage 1 is the Merkle log, signed checkpoints, relying-party pins and the tamper corpus; stage 2 adds RFC 3161 and OpenTimestamps anchors and witness cosigning, each only with an offline verification test and evidence that a time-stamp authority is available, otherwise dropped (a co-located witness gives no independence). One erasure protocol (request leaf, hold check, signed receipt, signer role `erasure`) records its method, one of `delete+salt-destroy`, `crypto_erase` or `tombstone`; the judge-path method is salt destruction plus delete, crypto-erasure through per-object keys is the stated upgrade, and the consequences are named states (section 11). A retention configuration (legal hold, retention period) is part of the ledger design, with a test that erased bodies leave verifiable receipts.
- **Envelope:** DSSE with an in-toto Statement and a SLSA-shaped decision predicate.
- **Standalone verifier in two parts.** The **core** (levels V0 to V4a: hashes, canonical form, ledger invariants, signatures against pins, certificate envelope checks; plus V5, the quote re-check against stored text, for parser-free locators) has zero dependencies, shares no server code, runs offline, validates bundle members with the restricted PSP schema dialect, and has two independent implementations tested against shared vectors. Anchor checks (V4b: RFC 3161 and OpenTimestamps, which need DER and CMS parsing) are a separate zero-dependency file. The parser-free locators are a raw byte range, a JSON pointer, a CSV cell and a text-layer range; V5 on a `textlayer` locator slices UTF-8 bytes and checks the code-point offset (section 7). The **recompute module** (V6 to V8: rule outcome, counterfactual and flip recomputation) interprets the policy IR as data and re-runs reconcile only from recorded link decisions and authority and date rules, never from fuzzy scores, so a second-language implementation cannot diverge at a threshold; it is budgeted at about 1,200 non-test lines per language (about 500 for the policy interpreter, about 700 for the reconcile check, NOT VERIFIED until built), recomputes only what is closed-form over recorded data, and checks producer-shipped witnesses for searches (a member with no witness format is INDETERMINATE, `V6.NO_WITNESS`); V6a recomputes feature values from recorded facts through declarative `FeatureProgram`s; each certificate states exactly which `V` it refers to. **Trust root:** PASS requires `--pins` supplied by the relying party; otherwise the verdict is INDETERMINATE (self-supplied trust). The verifier's digest is published out of band, not only inside the bundle. **Evidence mode:** `--evidence-dir` lets the relying party supply source bodies that must hash to the recorded digests (a stranger downloads the OFAC file themselves), so sources with parser-free locators can PASS offline without redistribution (a rights-gated PDF reaches V5 only through the stored text layer, because text extraction is parser code the core does not carry); the real-entity PASS bundle uses CC0 sources, and every synthetic list entry is labelled. Verifier verdicts are `PASS | FAIL | INDETERMINATE | NOT_APPLICABLE` with reason codes and a "what this does not prove" section; rights-gated hash-only bodies without `--evidence-dir` are INDETERMINATE.
- **Tamper corpus:** a generated set of valid bundles and labelled mutations (flip a byte in each member, drop the last ledger entries, swap an evidence blob, change a rule, reorder claims, replace a checkpoint, break canonical form, zip-slip paths, cassette edited without manifest update) used to mutation-test the verifier; published. Mutations include re-signing a bundle with a fresh key (expected INDETERMINATE without relying-party pins, and FAIL with `V2.SIGNER_NOT_PINNED` when the relying party pins keys), and structure-aware fuzzing covers mutations we did not think of. The reported figure is the false-accept rate **over the published mutation set**, not over all possible attacks, and one clean-room implementation of the verifier core is commissioned from the spec by a different author or model with no access to the first, because two implementations from the same team share failure modes.
- **Trace:** JSONL with one schema owned by M7 (hash chain `prevLineHash`, `why_kind`, `decider`, `phase`, `agent`, a closed `verb` vocabulary, `budget`, `tenant`), projected best-effort to OpenTelemetry GenAI conventions at a pinned version recorded as `semconv_version`; hash-only content by default, deterministic ids. Each decision carries `why_kind` (`rule`, `computed`, `model_reported` or `human_reported`); code steps carry structured reason codes (rule id, fact ids), and a linter requires a `ruleId` on `rule` lines and a registered function id on `computed` lines. ## 14a. M13 Case lifecycle, action broker and operations

A decision pipeline is not yet a product. Everything below attaches to one case, evidence and decision lifecycle, so it does not become a separate list of features.

- **Case lifecycle:** intake, assignment, evidence gathering, review, escalation, approval, execution, closure, appeal and reopening, as an explicit, event-sourced state machine (the case aggregate) whose transitions are trace events with `why`, are replayable and run on their own SLA clocks. A decision run ends at `decision.final`; approval waiting (hours, not minutes) is the case state `APPROVAL_PENDING` and not a runtime interrupt, and a changed world starts a fresh run.
- **Action broker (separate from the decision engine):** the only component allowed to cause an effect. It holds the per-case signed `ExecutionContract` (`act/po.release`), enforces expiring approvals, idempotency keys and independent confirmation of the release fields against the internal record, writes a **post-action receipt** into the case ledger, and supports reversal where the target system allows it (`TargetManifest.reversal` says which targets do; a reversal binding names the receipt it reverses and can only return funds to that receipt's origin). In the demo and in tests it drives a simulated payment system; real execution requires explicit configuration and approval.
- **Execution safety.** The contract is single-use: one port, `NonceStore.consumeAndRecordIntent` (owned by M13, with two implementations, a single-writer write-ahead log and an exclusive-create file), consumes the nonce and records the intent as one atomic entry, and a resume after a crash queries the target system with the idempotency key before acting. The broker verifies in **one order**: envelope and pinned key; audience (`broker:<tenant>:<adapter>:<restoreEpoch>`) and time window (`act/` contracts live 120 s); approvals (distinct subjects, segregation of duties, an assertion challenge derived from a request that covers the hash of the body without approvals, with a single-use ceremony nonce so that an old assertion cannot approve a re-issued request); kill and revocation state; world re-validation of the fact-set hash, list versions, vendor-master version and supersession (approvals can sit in a queue while the world changes, and a list update invalidates pending ones); fresh field equality against the ERP through the `broker` silo role; and last, the atomic consume and intent. Three clocks are separate: the request lifetime, the approval window and the contract TTL. The broker authenticates approvers and binds each approval to an identity-provider subject. The kill switch stops effects that have not crossed the send boundary; after it the broker reconciles and may propose a reversal. Signing goes through the one signer process (section 13); the capability policy is the conjunction of M9's inclusion proof of `approval.granted`, M8's check that each approval's assertion binds to the body, and M13's `G-FRESH` guard, which the case manager evaluates before it asks for a signature and the broker evaluates again at execution (the signer cannot read the ERP and is not given that power). Kill switch, approver authentication and key custody are components with test ids, not only residual-list entries.
- **Operational feedback:** record what happened after each decision (released and later reversed, held and later cleared, fraud confirmed), with the evidence for the outcome label and its trust level: an outcome label from an evidence-backed human finding is `VERIFIED`, and an unsupported human assertion is an `UNTRUSTED` note. Outcomes feed precedent weighting, the autonomy promotion statistics and the rule-proposal certificates; a label never comes from the deciding model.
- **Enterprise connectors:** ERP and vendor-master systems, contract repositories, approval systems, identity providers and payment operations, each a source channel or action target with the same contract tests.
- **Designed now, built after the core** (specified in the M13 blueprint; none is claimed until built and tested): tenant isolation (the tenant prefix is nevertheless in every store, key and ledger name from the first line of code, with one ledger tree per tenant), role-based access (14 RBAC roles, each mapped to one of the five silo roles) with policy ownership, retention and legal-hold settings, audit access for auditors, key management and rotation behind the signer, backup and recovery (with the restore epoch), continuous monitoring (portfolio re-screening and triggers over the same engines; repository depth, not demo content), cost and capacity tracking, connector health, upgrades, incident response, and data deletion as one governed erasure protocol (section 13).

Acceptance: lifecycle property tests (no skipped state, no execution without an approved and unexpired capability, idempotent retry of an action, a receipt recorded for every effect), a crash during execution resumes without a duplicate effect, and outcome labels never originate from the deciding model; the property suite is P1 to P22 with a TLA+ model of the broker protocol (M13 blueprint, component 21).

## 15. Agent roster (`prior-art/12` §8)

The `agent-behaviour-critic` runs early, not only at the end.

**Role-necessity ablation, pre-registered before any confirmatory run.** A run is confirmatory only if its pre-registration was publicly committed after 22 October 2026 00:00 UTC (or hash-anchored externally) before the run started, and the report header says so; any earlier run is exploratory by definition, because a local hash chain with a self-reported clock is not independent evidence of ordering. ## 16. Data and gold sets (`prior-art/15` §6)

Internal silos are synthetic and labelled (no real personal data): ERP vendor master, contracts, payments history, document lake of audits and certificates, employee-adjacent attributes for shell-vendor tests, generated from a seeded script with ground-truth labels. The generator reads M1's hashed silo contract (`contractHash` is recorded in its manifest) and emits data that conforms, plus planted violations declared as labelled deviations; it plants the write-provenance columns (`last_write_*`, `change_request_id`, `request_origin`, `editor_sub`), each bank-detail-change scenario family setting origin and editor. One generator, owned by M11, also produces the document corpus; M2 supplies its requirements (technique list, series, counts and the truth schema). The generator carries its own seeded random number generator as an explicit exception to "all randomness behind `ctx.rng`", with byte-equal regeneration as the substitute control (the statistics code of M11 is the second such exception, controlled by byte-equal recomputation of every report). Raw run stores live in `eval/runs/` and rendered reports in `eval/reports/`. Real public sources prove connectors on real entities; the internal side for those entities is synthetic and labelled so.

The gold set reflects ten domain requirements: sanctions name matching at regulator difficulty (perturbations modelled on the 2024 Finansinspektionen test: removed or replaced letters, altered dates of birth, plus transliteration, diacritics, word order, honorifics and legal-form suffixes; recall at a fixed alert budget, natural versus legal persons; baselines Watchman, yente, edit distance); negative names that look like positives; the full vendor bank-detail-change fraud family (lookalike domain, callback to the number in the request, change before a large payment, change by the same user who approves, legitimate bank mergers); split-invoice threshold evasion with aggregation window as a policy parameter; duplicate and shell vendors; three-way match with legitimate noise and fraud that matches perfectly; segregation of duties and collusion; ownership realism (holding chains, nominees, aggregation across owners, thresholds per regime as parameters, since the OFAC aggregation rule is not verified); time and staleness (release-time rescreening against a dated list version, phoenix companies); realistic prevalence (0.1, 1, 10 percent) with explicit asymmetric costs published as assumptions, plus pressure cases.

Gold labels separate evidence-silent, evidence-contradicts, different-entity, stale-update, extraction-error and equal-authority conflict, with claim-level attribution and an explicit origin DAG. To break circular evaluation (a rule-based reconciler defined by authority tiers wins on a generator whose truth is those tiers), the generator is specified and reviewed independently of the system code (a second human reviewer is a stated need; until a human signs, "reviewed independently" is `partial`), and the evaluation adds external anchors: published truth-discovery data and published entity-resolution benchmarks (availability partly verified; the truth-discovery datasets of Li et al. carry no stated terms), a Finansinspektionen-style perturbation test on real public list names against real baselines, and annotated natural conflicts. The natural-versus-legal-person result on real list names runs in the uncommitted tier and is published as aggregates, so a judge cannot reproduce it; the report says so and states where our method loses.

**Real public data, hybrid with planted cases (D-017 follow-up, 2026-10-15; licences NOT VERIFIED).** The backbone may use real public companies, registry records, filings and contracts, with planted forgeries, injections and bank-detail changes on top and a held-out test set produced by a separate process and never tuned against, which answers the circular-evaluation risk. Organizer rule: avoid real personal data, so real people never appear; person-name tests use synthetic names with the regulator-style perturbations. Each source needs its licence read before use (some sanctions datasets may be non-commercial; not checked) and a small cached sample so `SAMPLE_MODE` never depends on a live API.

## 17. M11 Evaluation (`prior-art/04` §10, `10` §C, `11` §9, `12` §9)

## 19a. Foundations bake-offs (decided by measurement before building at full scale)

Reusing mature infrastructure is preferred wherever it passes our guarantees; we own the distinctive parts (evidence ledger, reconciliation semantics, explanation certificates, trust lattice, decision-policy integration, evaluation). Each bake-off is a small spike with the same reference scenario for every candidate, a written decision rule, and a decision record in `docs/DECISIONS.md`. Results are published in `eval/reports/` whichever way they fall.

1. **Execution substrate.** Reference case: paused human approval, crash after an external effect, replay, retry under budget, failed approval gate. Candidates: a thin runtime of ours, LangGraph.js, XState and Temporal (only if judge-runnable with one command), with a judge-run screen pre-registered before any candidate runs (Temporal expected to fail it and XState to pass it; NOT VERIFIED). Decision rule: the candidate that passes the eight runtime acceptance tests (with our effect layer on top) with the least owned code and the fewest escapes of our guarantees. Bake-off 1 runs after the kernel contracts exist (build-order steps 1 to 3, including the write-ahead log, harness and broken drivers) because candidates are judged with our effect layer on top; "least owned code" is counted as non-test lines; no outcome is assumed, and a hybrid is acceptable if it wins.
2. **Policy engine.** Encode the same 30 procurement rules, including a cumulative-exposure rule and an obligation, in Cedar, OPA/Rego and our policy IR. Compare expressiveness, provability (Z3 or Cedar SymCC), evaluation speed, TypeScript fit and owned lines. Verifier-interpretability is a gating criterion, not a tie-breaker: a candidate that cannot be interpreted by a dependency-free evaluator in two languages (about 500 lines each, with differential vectors) cannot be the normative policy format of a bundle. Adopt Cedar or OPA if they cover the required properties and pass that gate; if one wins on expressiveness but fails it, adopt it as an authoring front-end or CI cross-check (as Cedar SymCC is in section 10), keep our IR as the bundle format, and state the cost. The bake-off runs when its 30-rule corpus is written, after the M5a types.
3. **Entity matching and clustering.** Splink, yente, nomenklatura and our cascade, plus exact correlation clustering with hard cannot-link constraints (ILP or an exact solver on the small components left after blocking, deterministic tie-breaks, a deterministic fallback; no randomised rounding in the judged path) as an alternative to the current constrained clustering, judged on false-merge rate under adversarial bridge entities (one planted entity linking two valid clusters) on the same planted-pair gold set, including the near-duplicate class ("Series A" versus "Series B"). Decision rule: false-merge rate first, then recall at a fixed review budget. It needs the planted-pair gold set, so it runs after build-order step 11. OpenSanctions data is used only where its licence permits.
4. **PDF parsing.** The locator-fidelity benchmark of section 7 across pdf.js, PDFium (including `@embedpdf/pdfium`), Docling Parse and the oracle, after the document corpus exists (build-order step 11); the primary metric is the silent-wrong locator rate.
5. **Embedded store for synthetic silos and source tables.** `node:sqlite` (experimental on Node 22) versus DuckDB (native or WASM), judged on judge-run weight, install size, determinism, SQL parity with the Zetaris path (Zetaris lists DuckDB among its engines) and bitemporal query ergonomics. The World Model remains the only store of reconciled facts; the embedded SQL store holds raw source tables, so there are not two truth stores. The judge path default is a file-backed append-only store; `node:sqlite` and DuckDB are candidates for evaluation tooling and raw source tables, decided here.
6. **Meterless engine strategy.** For each engine, run our extended suites (every clause the conformance runner does not assert) against the official TypeScript reference first. The decision rule is the measured one in the M12 blueprint (C6), with a from-spec implementation as the tie default, and not "passes most": a reference that meets it is forked with attribution and its documented gaps fixed; one that fails structurally (hash "embeddings", non-replayable fact resolution) is replaced by an implementation from the spec, keeping the reference as a differential oracle. The engine requirements R-WM1 to R-WM4 (World Model) and the H-MEM requirements from M6 are part of the suites. Either way the vendored conformance runners and the per-engine ablation stand.
7. **Structured output.** Whether the Nemotron endpoint offers schema-constrained decoding (NOT VERIFIED); if so, measure parse-failure rate and any quality loss against prompt-only JSON. Validation by our schemas applies regardless; constrained decoding only reduces rejections.

9. **Explanation oracle.** An SMT or MaxSAT encoding of the fusion step and the policy IR, from which abductive (minimal robustly sufficient) and contrastive (minimal flip) explanations are computed by a solver, as a second independent implementation of V and a possible fast path beside the component-tabulation enumeration of M4 (which stays the dependency-free path the offline verifier can check). Decision rule: the encoding must agree with the enumeration on every test ledger, and it replaces nothing unless it is faster at realistic ledger sizes without adding a verifier dependency.
8. **Evaluation toolchain tier.** Non-Node tools (clingo, Splink, Watchman, Docling, Inspect AI, promptfoo) run from a pinned container or lockfile with committed expected outputs. A judge needs only Node; a reproducer needs the evaluation tier. Disk is budgeted per candidate before it is installed.

9. **Console view layer.** An owned minimal builder over native ES modules versus Lit versus Preact plus htm. Criteria: no raw-HTML sink in the builder's API, bytes shipped, lines owned, ARIA and keyboard ergonomics.

Order (section 20): bake-offs 1, 5 and 8 at step 3a; 2 once its rule corpus exists; 3 and 4 after step 11; 6 in step 10; 7 when a key exists; 9 with M10.

Rejected as core techniques, with reasons: Myers diff or other tolerant alignment as *quote verification* (it could pass a near-fabricated quote; our exact, normalised-with-index-map and near-as-warning statuses stay, and a diff library is used only to report disagreement between two extractors); copying SAFE's prompts or the Generative Agents retrieval formula (our design differs on purpose, see `prior-art/04` and `05`; we cite them and write our own); two competing truth stores (a temporal knowledge graph such as Graphiti is a behavioural baseline for bitemporal tests, not a second store).

## 21. Closures from the demand traceability matrix

`docs/design/01-demand-traceability-matrix.md` traced 178 organizer demands against spec v2 (111 designed, 60 partial, 7 gaps, from 26 root causes); the refresh against v3 and the Official Rules has 203 rows (153 designed, 43 partial, 7 gaps). The architecture changes are folded in here; ids F-* refer to its fix list.

**Pipeline and behaviour**
- **F-REPLAN, evidence-gap re-plan loop.** After M4, a code-owned coverage check reads the gap ledger. If a sub-question is unanswered and an unqueried channel or document exists, it routes to `planner.replan` with a built `why` (for example "sub-question S3 unanswered; channel courtlistener unqueried"). The planner amends the plan for that sub-question only, source agents fetch under a budget slice, M3 and M4 recompute on the delta (assertions are idempotent). `maxVisits: 2`; on exhaustion the gap stays in the ledger and caps confidence. The organizers draw this loop as their model strong design; it also gives the low-coverage branch of conditional branching a target. Test: first pass lacks evidence, ledger changes after re-plan, cap holds, exhaustion keeps the gap; trace rule id `coverage.replan`.
- **F-DISCOVER, source discovery as an explicit traced step.** `plan.source_routing` lists, per sub-question, candidate channels from the per-attribute authority table, `canHandle` and probe status, chosen and rejected channels with a `why`. A `request_more` edge from M3 to the source agents fires when a conflict is unresolved and a higher-authority source has not been queried (reuses the F-REPLAN machinery).
- **F-STAGES, per-stage entry points.** `plumbline reconcile | explain | decide`, each consuming the previous stage's typed, hashed output from a bundle and writing its own artefact and trace, so each track is judged alone. A question-template registry in M4 offers at least three bounded questions (release safety, ownership chain, sanctions exposure). Test: each stage alone from a bundle yields an output hash equal to the in-pipeline hash.
- **Reflexion limits reconciled.** The critic rubric gets one advisory revision (organizer pairing), separate from reflexion; deterministic-failure reflexion (rule engine rejects the action, validator fails, human rejects) gets at most two retries, then escalation.

**Runtime and judge experience**
- **F-BUDGET, per-phase budgets.** The run wall clock is sliced into the organizer's seven phases (startup, data loading, planning, retrieval and analysis, critique, final result, logging) as stage budgets with a finalize reserve; a documents budget and case-intake caps; L3 and L4a/b routed by claim criticality with memoised judgements; separate replay (judge) and live profiles. ### 21a-3. Lessons from OpenClaw (D-020, 2026-10-15; README, VISION and docs pages read, code not read)

OpenClaw (`openclaw/openclaw`, MIT, TypeScript, about 392k stars, 82k forks, created 2025-11-24, pushed 2026-10-15) is the reference for a general agent that acts. Its Gateway is a local control plane for sessions, tools, events and channel connections; it separates a trusted Gateway from untrusted, movable execution and states that policy is enforced in code, not requested in a prompt. It lists seven testable properties of an enterprise harness: separated trust boundary, policy as code, authenticated access with bounded roles, secrets with owners, versioned state with guarded upgrades, recorded provenance, and independent stewardship. It ships about 180 extensions and 50 skills through a plugin SDK and a registry (ClawHub), 20+ messaging channels, companion apps, a one-line installer with an onboarding wizard, `doctor --fix` migrations, release channels and signed releases, and a foundation (501(c)(3)) as steward. Its own docs say sandboxing is off by default, and open issues in Hermes Agent show approvals covering shell strings only.

| Lesson | Plumbline change | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| Distribute as a plugin where the users already are | Ship the gate as an OpenClaw extension and ClawHub skill, and as plugins for Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and Hermes, beside the MCP server | a stranger installs and gates a real tool call in one line in each host |
| One-line install, onboarding wizard, daemon status, dashboard | `plumbline onboard`, `plumbline status`, `plumbline dashboard` | timed clean-machine run |
| Config compatibility discipline: no silent aliases, `doctor --fix` migrates and backs up | Versioned state and config with owned migrations and a `doctor` command | upgrade test across two schema versions |
| Seven enterprise properties as a public checklist | Adopt them as acceptance tests and add two of our own: verifiable decisions (offline verifier) and tested learning (replay plus symbolic check) | each property has a test that can fail |
| Lean core, plugins carry the growth ("per-call tax"); recurring demand defines an interface | Keep the kernel small; sources, packs and channels are plugins against stable contracts | a pack added without touching the kernel |
| Approvals where people already are | Approve or hold from Slack, Teams, email and chat, bound to the exact request digest | an approval from a chat button releases once and cannot be replayed |
| Skills that operate the system itself | Maintenance skills for diagnosing and configuring Plumbline | a skill diagnoses a planted misconfiguration |
| Neutral stewardship, signed releases, public security record | Permissive licence, signed releases, SECURITY.md, no separate enterprise edition | release signature verifies |

### 21a-4. Operating-layer view and corrected differentiators (D-021, 2026-10-15)

**Operating-layer view.** The product is delivered as an operating layer for agent work with the verified kernel at its centre: agents are processes in a live table; every consequential tool call is a system call the kernel decides (evidence, policy, approval bound to the request, one-time release); the case file and memory are the filesystem (World Model, H-MEM, replayable history); source channels and domain packs are drivers that can be toggled as layers; watch mode is the scheduler; a mission-control console (sources as layers, entities as nodes, conflicts in red, an action queue, a time slider that replays a decision) is the shell; learning is the override-to-rule loop with replay and a symbolic check. Pattern source: the live-data globe `bilawalsidhu/gods-eye-view` (about 56.8k stars on 2026-10-15) spread through one fused visual surface, real data, near-zero setup, toggleable layers and community-added packs.

**Corrected differentiators** (answers from DeepWiki, an AI index of each repository, 22 repositories, four fixed questions; leads to confirm in code, full answers in `docs/design/prior-art/17-claim-matrix-from-deepwiki.md`):
- Approval bound to the exact request is NOT unique: Hermes Agent (request id plus digest), Mastra's security-incident-triage template (action and decision fingerprints in a resume token) and n8n (checkpoint token) were reported to have it. It is table stakes, implemented but not headlined. Vectimus (Cedar policy gate for coding agents) was reported not to bind approvals to the payload.
- Quote evidence is partial elsewhere: Hermes has a grounded-citations skill that verifies verbatim quotes. Ours adds exact offsets in a hashed text layer and offline verification.
- Replay exists as evaluation: Mastra's template runs an offline replay of policy over a 72-case approved dataset and publishes CI gates with numerators, denominators, corpus hashes and tamper checks (read in its `docs/evals.md`). That is the bar our published scores must meet or exceed.
- Not seen in any answer or file read: an override that becomes a rule only after replay plus a symbolic check of the permissive surface, and exact minimal-evidence and minimal-flip counterfactuals computed by re-running the policy. These remain the claims to demonstrate.
- Learned memory or skill changes tested before they take effect: not confirmed for Hermes (background review and a write approval, no replay), mem0, Letta or Hindsight; the YES answers for n8n, RAGFlow, cognee and claude-mem concern evaluation of their own products, not gating of learned changes.
