## 2. The problem, the users and the promise

**Problem (a premise to test, NOT VERIFIED until practitioner interviews).** Releasing money to a wrong or misrepresented counterparty, or changing bank details on a forged request, is a recurring and irreversible failure; the inputs plausibly conflict (internal records, public registries, documents) and nobody reconciles identity facts at the moment of release. As AI agents start to take irreversible actions (pay, deploy, delete, send), the same gap opens in every domain. Defensible figures and their definitions are in spec section 1; there is no primary-source cost figure for wrong-counterparty release.

**Hero user (D-029).** The head of risk and operations at a mid-size health insurer (a payer), working one Decision Desk. **Earlier users, kept as packs.** The accounts-payable approver who releases a payment or accepts a bank-detail change; a developer who runs coding agents and wants a gate before they push, deploy or delete. **Platform user.** An owner of many agents who wants a live table of what each agent is allowed and has done.

**Promise in one sentence (organizer format).** An agent that helps the head of risk and operations at a mid-size health insurer decide whether to release or hold each consequential action (a prior-authorization approval, a claim, a provider payment, a contract renewal, an access or deletion request) by reconciling the clinical, policy, claims and billing records that disagree, verifying each claim against the source text, applying the plan's policy and precedent, and producing a signed decision dossier with cited evidence, the exact criterion behind any hold, the one fact that would flip it and an approval state, and that turns the approver's overrides into rules only after they pass a replay on past cases and a symbolic check. The system never denies on its own: it approves or routes to a clinician. All health content is synthetic.

**Positioning.** An operating system for decisions that are costly if wrong, with a verified kernel: every consequential action is a system call the kernel decides, and the result is a record a stranger can verify offline. Submission track: Track 3, Reasoning Architecture (D-028); one primary track is judged and cross-track coverage is not scored twice, so the demo is framed in Track 3's own words. The same kernel serves other packs and sits in front of other agents as an MCP proxy and SDK (grant story). Not positioned as an observability or evaluation tool. Demand evidence: `prior-art/2026-10-17-market-and-demand-evidence.md`; best-in-world picks: `prior-art/2026-10-17-best-in-world-check-kernel-parts.md`.

**The three doors (v3.0, D-035).** One engine, three ways in. Developers get the `prove-it` skill, a local MCP server and a Cursor rule (F-D01 to F-D05). Everyday people get a page and a browser button that X-rays any AI answer or denial letter (F-D06, F-D07). Insurers and hospitals get the Decision Desk (F-S01 to F-S23 and the P rows). The face is an X-ray and a time machine for AI answers: every claim lights up against its exact source quote, a what-if control flips one fact and shows the decision change, and the record verifies offline. Evidence for the doors and for each row is in `prior-art/2026-10-19-top-repos-pattern-study.md`.

## 6. Success measures (all to be measured; none exists yet)

Decision quality on the gold set (false-release and false-hold rates with intervals, not a single accuracy figure); citation recall and precision; quote-check coverage; contradiction-detection recall and false-contradiction rate; false-merge count on planted near-duplicates; the override-to-rule loop's acceptance and rejection behaviour on planted rules; consolidation-harm curve (memory on versus off, constrained versus unconstrained); crash-and-resume exactly-once; offline verifier PASS rate on a fresh machine; time of a clean-room run; judge-simulator score against the Official rubric. Targets are set in the pre-registration file before any confirmatory run.

## 7. Out of scope or deferred (with reasons)

Legal conclusions on sanctions, GDPR Article 22 and AI Act scoping (a lawyer is needed, D8); a hosted multi-tenant service; a forensic layer for forged images and handwriting (stated limit in 9a); owner-operator persona (evidence is anecdotal); real customer data; a separate enterprise edition; claims about human oversight efficacy before participants have run (D9); the hosted Meterless runtime until the sponsor answers.
